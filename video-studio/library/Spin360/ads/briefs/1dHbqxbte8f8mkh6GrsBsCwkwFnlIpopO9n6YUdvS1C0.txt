[4] SCRIPT

I never used to think about the air in my baby’s room 


But then I found a study that honestly stopped me in my tracks.


Researchers found that babies sleeping in rooms with a fan had dramatically lower rates of sudden infant death syndrome. Not 5%.




Not 10%. More than 70% lower. So why was that? The researchers didn't think it was the fan itself. They thought it was the air. And that's when a strange pattern started showing up everywhere. I found things like open air schools in the early 1900s. Parents in Sweden who have their babies sleep outside an ancient Japanese forest. Hospitals where patients heal in fresh moving air.




For hundreds of years, doctors, parents and researchers kept stumbling onto the same idea. Humans seem to do better when air keeps moving. So when I came across David, who had spent four years obsessed with this exact problem, I paid attention. What he built is honestly hard to explain until you feel it. It pulls that cool air from the floor and the warm, stale air from the ceiling, and sends it out in every direction all at once, like a breeze that wraps around you from every direction on a perfect cool night.




The first time I slept with it, it felt like the walls of my bedroom just weren't there anymore. And I actually woke up cold in the middle of the summer on the lower settings. I don't hear it at all. Just fresh air moving through my room all night. Then you drop in a scent pod and 10s later. Your whole room smells like a forest pine, eucalyptus, cedar and in the summer you can use citronella to keep bugs away.




It's that same amazing airflow. Humans have been trying to replicate for thousands of years. It's called the spin 360, and they're offering $110 off this summer only.
