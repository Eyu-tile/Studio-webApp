	Strategy
# Element
	Answer
	Desire
	Wake up actually rested. Sleep in air that moves all night, like sleeping outside, without a fan blasting you in the face.
	Avatar
	Adults 40-65 (mostly women) who sleep with the door and window shut, sleep eight hours and still wake up groggy, and already tried a regular fan (in the face all night, or pointed away and useless).
	Angle
	The Debate: "Who sleeps better, a Danish baby or you?" The baby napping outside in the snow wins, because outside the air never stops moving. Your sealed bedroom air doesn't move, so you breathe your own breath back in all night.
	Mechanism
	Spin360 pulls air in from the top and the bottom and sends it out 360 degrees, so the whole room moves gently, not one spot. A soft breeze, not a blast.
	Proof
	The smoke test (smoke pulled in top and bottom, out all around), ribbons and tissue moving all around the fan, two real 5-star reviews (Judge.me, verbatim).
	Authority
	David Patrick: four years on one blade, prototype after prototype, tested on a power drill with smoke. Same guy who invented the Shark Wheel and got a deal on Shark Tank.
	Awareness
	Hook: Unaware (a question about who sleeps better, no product). Body: problem (sealed room, groggy mornings, fans don't fix it), then solution and product (Spin360). Ends Product Aware with a 30-night trial.
	Emotion
	Surprise ("the baby in the snow wins?"), recognition ("that's me, groggy every morning"), relief and calm (the soft breeze), desire (waking up rested).
	Format
	Debate VSL in the format of the cat reel "Who has a better life, cats or dogs?": real phone footage, a hard cut every 2-3 s, one big word on screen at a time, guarantee end card. Visual pool = our winning Swedish Babies ad + UGC + a few AI shots.
	Hooks
	2 verbal hooks x 2 visual hooks = 4 ads. Verbal 1: "Who sleeps better: a Danish baby, or you?" Verbal 2: "Who sleeps better: a baby napping in the snow, or you in your warm bed?" Visual A = match cut (baby asleep, cut to you awake). Visual B = split screen (baby on top, you below, your half goes cold).
	Offer
	30-night trial: "If your bedroom doesn't feel like a window's open, send it back." No price, no discount, no "money-back" wording.

Pacing and sound:
1. Hard cut every 2-3 seconds, like the cat reel. Never hold a shot longer than 3 s. Exceptions: the smoke test (up to 4 s, because the viewer must see smoke enter top AND bottom), the two review cards, and the end card (3+ s hold). The dust-mote sequence (rows 10-12) is three separately generated framings, so it cuts like everything else.
2. Cut on VO beats. Each new sentence starts on a new shot. Inside a sentence, cut on the commas (example: 'In strollers, / bundled up, / even in the snow.').
3. Everything must look like real phone footage: handheld, natural light, slight noise, no glossy commercial grade. AI shots are graded down to match the real UGC around them (a touch of grain, less contrast, slightly underexposed at night).
4. Color story: Danish outdoor shots are cold white/natural daylight. The 'sealed box' and problem section is dim, blue-grey, flat and lifeless. Product and result shots are warm lamp light plus the Spin360's blue LED. The end card is clean and bright.
5. Sound design, low under the VO (about -25 dB): wind and snow outside, a clear door-latch click on 'Door shut', a window-handle clunk on 'Window shut', near silence on the 8-hours shot and on 'It's the opposite', the alarm buzz on both alarm shots, an ugly fan roar on the 'blast' shots, the drill whine on the drill shot, soft rustling trees on 'wind through trees'. Same soft music bed and the same female AI narrator as Swedish V2.
6. THE SHARED BODY STARTS AT 'Danish babies nap outside.' (row 1). 'Sadly, it's not you.' (row 0) belongs to each hook's bridge, so a hook test only changes hook + bridge and the body is byte-identical for H1 and H2.
7. Callbacks the editor must keep exact: (a) the snooze shot (rows 13-14) and the rested-alarm shot (rows 32-33) use the SAME nightstand, phone, framing and camera position; only the light and the person change. (b) The 'Researchers' baby in the fur hood is used ONLY in the hooks and in the row 51 match cut, so the ending bookends the opener. All other body baby close-ups use different babies (A7 in rows 2 and 35, A9 in rows 4 and 20). (c) One AI 'YOU' woman (about 45, reference still A1) is used in H1 shot 2, the H2 bottom half and every AI adult shot in the body that is not built from a pool frame (rows 18, 28, 31, 32-33, 51-52, 55), so 'you' stays the same person and the same age.
8. Product order (11.m04): no Spin360 on screen from 'Sadly' until 'That's what Spin360 does.' Problem cuts first, product and mechanism in the middle, clean product plus guarantee seal at the end.
9. Baby safety: every baby shot shows a bundled, calm, sleeping baby, and the first body shot after either hook is a parent with a stroller (row 1). Porch and garden strollers at a home are fine. Never a crying baby, never one that looks abandoned, never the baby lying on the snow, never the 7-Eleven / shop-front stroller.
