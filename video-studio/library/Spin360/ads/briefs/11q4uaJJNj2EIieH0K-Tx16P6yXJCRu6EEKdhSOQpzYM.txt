Strategy
# Element
	Answer
	1 · Desire
	"I want my brain to actually relax when I go to bed." Not knocked out — relieved. Real sleep, wake up feeling like a person again, patience for the people she loves.
	2 · Avatar
	Experiences: "tired but wired" — exhausted, but the second her head hits the pillow her brain switches on: tomorrow's to-dos, work, conversations, something awkward from years ago. Behaviors: sleep apps, podcasts, a fan even in winter. Emotions: exhausted, frustrated, quietly blaming herself. Cast: a woman ~38 (the singer's voice is early 30s) and her husband.
	3 · Angle
	Quiet room, loud brain. It's not you, it's the quiet: in dead silence your own thoughts become the loudest thing in the room.
	4 · Mechanism
	One steady sound for your mind, a soft breeze for your body. The brain needs one steady sound to hold on to. Apps loop, podcasts pull you in, a regular fan blasts air at you. Spin360 makes real white noise that never changes, and was made to send a soft breeze around the room that never hits your face.
	5 · Authority
	Researchers at Harvard (the "would rather take a shock than sit alone with their thoughts" study, Science 2014) · thousands of people sleeping with it · reviews · 30-night trial.
	6 · Awareness
	Problem aware: she knows her brain won't shut off; she doesn't know the silence is part of why. Many are already solution aware (apps, podcasts, fans) — the song handles that with "they're on the right track, but…".
	7 · Emotion
	Opens: negative and funny ("that's me"). Middle: frustration → guilt (next day) → relief ("it's not you") → "I've tried everything" → hope at the product. Ends: calm, happy, singalong. The song gets brighter the moment the product appears.
	8 · Positioning
	Enemy: the dead-silent bedroom while your brain is still running — and the things that don't fix it (looping apps, podcasts, a fan blasting air). Spin360: real white noise + a soft breeze around the room.
	Production brief
Format: AI singing ad (the whole ad is the song). Trim the first 1.6 s of the song file so her voice starts at 0:00; final length ≈ 2:58. All timecodes below are after the trim. 3 ads = the same song and body with 3 different visual hooks (first ~7 s). From "Head hits the pillow…" on, the three ads are identical.
Look: 3D animation in the style of a Pixar movie for the whole ad. Vertical 9:16. Lyrics on screen word by word (bold uppercase, the sung word highlighted), plus a few on-screen text lines marked in the scene descriptions.
The big idea — LOUD vs QUIET: her thoughts are a character: THE BRAIN, a small pink cartoon brain with a megaphone. Before the product: dark, cold blue light, tight framing, the Brain screaming, thoughts filling the room. After the product: warm amber light, open framing, a soft breeze, the Brain holding on to one steady line of sound and finally falling asleep. The flip happens on the instrumental break (1:18–1:26), which is where Spin360 appears.
Motifs that tie every scene together (no random shots): (1) the Brain — megaphone → lowers it → holds the steady line → asleep hugging a tiny pillow; (2) the color — cold blue → warm amber; (3) mirrors — 3 coffees → 1 coffee, the clock at 1:07 AM → the clock barely moved, the face blasted by a fan → her calm face with a breeze, the overhead shot packed with thoughts → the same shot empty and calm, "QUIET ROOM. LOUD BRAIN." → "STEADY SOUND. QUIET BRAIN."
Cast & set (same in every scene): SHE (~38, brown messy bun, grey sleep t-shirt, plaid pajama pants), HIM (her husband, dark hair, stubble, navy t-shirt, asleep and snoring while she's awake), THE BRAIN, one bedroom. Character sheet below.
Product: Spin360, white floor model only (reference: FLOOR_0.1.jpg). Always on the floor next to her side of the bed, never on furniture. The air is a breeze, not wind: a couple of strands of hair and the curtain hem move, nothing more. Regular-fan scenes show OTHER people only, generic fans, no brands. No app or podcast logos.
Music: the song is the only audio. There is no hard stop: the song has an ~8 s instrumental break at 1:18–1:26 — that is the product reveal. Optional light sound design only (megaphone squeal, snore, clock ticks), never over the vocal.
Research proof: at "Researchers at Harvard…", put a real screenshot of the study page over the scene (Science, 2014, "Just think: The challenges of the disengaged mind," Wilson et al.). Do not invent a card.
