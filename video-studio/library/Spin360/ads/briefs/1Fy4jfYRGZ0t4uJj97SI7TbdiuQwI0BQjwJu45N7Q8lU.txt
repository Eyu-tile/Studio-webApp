1. WHAT THIS AD IS
A guy tells you a true internet story: neighbors called the cops on a husky sleeping in the snow — and the dog was fine. He just loves it out there. Then the story turns to YOU: your body also has a perfect place, and this machine brings it into your bedroom.


It should feel like a viral story video, not an ad. Think of those channels that retell a true story over real phone footage. The viewer should not smell "commercial" until the very end.


________________


2. THE BIG PICTURE (read this before cutting)
The video goes from COLD to WARM. That's the whole idea, told with color:


* First half: snow, white and blue, real phone videos. Cold and alive.
* Second half: bedroom, amber and soft light. Warm and calm.
* The edit literally "brings the outside inside" — same as the story.


One rule above all: every line of VO gets its own picture. When he says "front door open" — we SEE a front door open. When he says "the grin" — we SEE a husky grinning. If the words and the picture don't match, the viewer scrolls.


Pace: fast cuts in the story half (2–3 seconds per shot). Slow down in the product half (3–5 seconds per shot). The story is energy. The product is calm.

4. IMAGE TECHNIQUES (use these — they make it feel professional)
You have both videos AND still images in the links folder. Stills are not a problem — they're a tool:


* Photo burst: 3–4 stills back to back, super fast (~10–15 frames each). Great for lists ("door / bed / dog") and energy moments.
* Slow push: slow zoom into a still image (2–4 seconds). Makes photos feel alive and serious. Use on the tweet, the memory shots, the living room.
* Snap zoom: fast punch-in on a video for emphasis. Use on jokes and reveals, max 2–3 times total.
* Freeze frame: stop the video for half a second on a funny beat ("with no zipper"). Once or twice only.
* Match cut: two shots that rhyme (warm room → cold field). This is the classiest move in the video — use it at section 6.
* Music dip: lower the music so a sound can play (the "shhh", the wind). Sound sells feeling.


Don't stack all techniques everywhere. One trick per moment. Clean beats clever.


________________


5. MUSIC
* ONE track for the whole video. Calm, warm, a little wonder. NO random dramatic violin, no epic trailer music.
* The drop lands at the story→product turn: right after "...inside a closed-up house." and before "So one engineer in California..." — the pause is already built into the VO.
* Final track comes later via the music workflow — cut with a temp track at low volume for now.
6. TEXT ON SCREEN
* Minimal. Caption-style only (small, clean, bottom third). No headlines shouting at the viewer.
* Real screenshots (tweet, reviews) do the "text" work — they're proof, not decoration.
* NEVER: red X's, black-and-white "before" comparisons, arrows, flashing text.
7. HARD RULES 
1. The dog must NEVER look like he's suffering. Nordic breeds only, relaxed, door open, owner around. It's a choice, not abandonment.
2. No TikTok watermarks in paid media. 
3. The snow must look GORGEOUS. It's August — snow is the coolness the viewer is craving.
4. VO words are locked. Cut picture to the words, never the other way.
