Use mostly this footage: Erin Schneider
The visual direction should sell the feeling first, not the product.
Use mostly UGC / lifestyle footage. The goal is to make the home feel more elevated, fresh, and expensive without making the ad look too polished or commercial.
* Hook: Start with a beautiful but believable home/apartment shot — something that instantly feels clean, elevated, and aspirational.
* “Easy fix”: Show someone naturally interacting with the Spin360 or placing the scent pod in.
* Mechanism: When we talk about moving air around the room, show the product working and subtle signs of airflow — curtains, hair, fabric, or a plant moving gently.
* “Expensive hotel smell”: Focus on atmosphere. Clean bedroom, made bed, warm lighting, premium-looking details, someone walking into the room or relaxing in the space.
* “Not just a diffuser”: Bring the product back in more clearly here and show that it’s actually moving air around the room.
* Breeze / white noise: Show someone relaxing, lying in bed, reading, or just enjoying the room while the air moves softly around them.
* CTA: Finish with a clean hero shot of Spin360 naturally placed in the room.
Overall, think 70–80% feeling / lifestyle / UGC and 20–30% product demonstration.
We’re selling: “This makes my home feel more expensive.”
The scent pod and airflow are the mechanism that explains why.
