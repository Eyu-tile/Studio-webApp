Strategy
# Element
	Answer
	1 · Desire
	"I want to fall asleep easily, even when my mind won't stop." Why train: fall asleep without lying awake thinking → more continuous sleep → wake up rested → more energy, patience and clarity. Core: rest / relief / control.
	2 · Avatar
	Physically tired at night, but the brain switches back on the moment they get into bed: replaying conversations, tomorrow, to-do lists, work, money, family, random memories. Small night sounds stand out (a car, a creak, a partner moving). Many already put on podcasts, rain, brown noise, TV or apps. Core experience: "My body is tired, but my brain won't shut off."
	3 · Angle
	fall asleep without fighting your thoughts — one steady sound your brain can settle into, instead of a podcast or TV that gives it something new to think about.  When your bedroom gets completely quiet, everything else feels louder. Even your own thoughts.
	4 · Mechanism
	A steady sound for your mind. A soft breeze for your body. The steady, predictable fan sound gives the mind one simple, non-demanding thing to settle into (no story to follow like TV, no loop like an app) and softens the little night noises. Then the airflow: pulls air from the top and bottom and moves a soft breeze around the room, no direct blast. In this ad the SOUND leads; the breeze comes second.
	5 · Awareness
	Problem aware → solution aware. She knows the problem; many already use background sound. We connect both to a better version: one steady sound + a whole room that feels calm.
	6 · Emotion
	  

Opens: negative, medium ("I'm exhausted. Why am I still thinking?"). Mid: frustration with the things that didn't work → curiosity at the sister's → the click. Ends: relief, calm, surrender — "at some point I'm just asleep."
	7 · Positioning
	Enemy: a dead-quiet, still bedroom while your brain is running; secondary: solutions that are stimulating, looped or need attention (podcast, TV, apps). Reframe: you don't have to force your brain to stop — give it somewhere to settle. Line: "A steady sound for your mind. A soft breeze for your body."
	Production brief
Format: first-person animated story in the style of the Pack 'Em backpack ad (reference: uhSBvnTUIagkLCqy.mp4


 Same storytelling engine: repeated situation → frustration → things she tried → discovery through someone else → product → the same night, now solved → close. ~1:50 VO, calm female voice ~40, American, conversational, 4th-grade reading level. 3 visual hooks with the SAME VO line.
Look (copy the reference style, not its setting): stylized 3D animated characters (Disney/Pixar-like: big expressive eyes, long neck, exaggerated tired expressions) inside richly detailed, realistic 3D interiors with warm cinematic light. Our world is home at night: warm lamp light + cool blue moonlight; daytime/sister scenes warm like the reference. The Spin360 is always photoreal and exactly on-model (white base, slim pole, clear slatted head, gold ring, white cap — no blades), always on the floor. Product close-ups with hands, like the reference. Captions: one word at a time, white bold, centered.
Central visual idea: her thoughts are visible — glossy 3D icons (email, dollar signs, calendar, sticky note, a dinner-party bubble, an old photo) swirling around her head. When the Spin360 turns on, soft pale-blue sound ripples fill the room, the icons slow down, freeze and dissolve. In the mechanism beat: chaotic zigzag icons vs ONE smooth steady wave.
