	Strategy (same for Body 1 and Body 2)
# Element
	Answer
	1 · Desire
	"I want my brain to actually relax when I go to bed." Not knocked out — relieved. Get real sleep, wake up feeling like a person again, have patience for the people she loves and energy for work.
	2 · Avatar
	Experiences: "tired but wired" — her body is exhausted, but the second she lies down her brain switches on: replaying conversations, work, something embarrassing from years ago, tomorrow's to-do list. Behaviors: scrolls her phone to distract herself, falls asleep to podcasts or sleep-sound recordings, or can't sleep without a fan, even in winter. Emotions: exhausted, frustrated, quietly blaming herself ("something's wrong with me"). Demographic: US women ~40–60 (cast ~45).
	3 · Angle
	Your brain gets louder when your room goes quiet. It's not you, it's the quiet: in dead silence your own thoughts become the loudest thing in the room.
	4 · Mechanism
	A steady sound for your mind, a soft breeze for your body. The brain needs one steady sound to lock on to. Recordings stop, loop, or pull you in; a regular fan rattles, hums and blasts air at you. Spin360 makes real white noise — one steady sound that never changes — and pulls air in from the top and the bottom and pushes a soft breeze around the whole room.
	5 · Authority
	Sleep researchers (Body 2 opens with it; Body 1 says it in the body) · thousands of people sleeping with it every night · reviews · 30-night guarantee.
	6 · Awareness
	Problem aware: she knows her brain won't shut off; she doesn't know the silence is part of why. Many are already solution aware (fans, white noise, podcasts) — the body handles that with "they're on the right track, but…".
	7 · Emotion
	Opens: negative, high (shock, "that's me"). Middle: frustration → guilt (next day) → relief ("it's not you") → "I've tried everything" → hope at the product. Ends: very calm, positive (surrender, a good morning, relief). High intensity in the problem, very calm in the resolution — the contrast is the ad.
	8 · Positioning
	Enemy: the dead-silent bedroom while your brain is still running — and the things that don't fix it (phone recordings, podcasts, a regular fan blasting air). Spin360: real white noise + a soft breeze around the room. Something for your mind to settle into, something for your body to settle into.
	Production brief
Inspiration: https://app.trendtrack.io/en/w-gabriels-workspace-7jyN37H/explorer?tab=ads&ad=facebook_1365634045642067&adSource=meta


Format: "Here's what happens, minute by minute" night timeline, ~2:20–2:30 VO. This tab: 2 ads (Body 1 × 2 visual hooks). Both ads use the same VO and on-screen text; only the first 3–5 seconds of picture change. From "In the first few minutes…" on, the two ads are identical.
Look: cinematic, natural, realistic live-action (real people, real skin, lamp light at night, clear daylight by day; never glossy-commercial) cut with medical-realistic 3D inserts inside the ear and the brain at every mechanism beat.
The big idea — thoughts made visible: her thoughts leave her head as amber points of light and project onto the ceiling she's staring at. The Spin360 sound is a smooth blue wave that crosses the room, enters the ear and dims the amber. Amber = racing thoughts / anxiety. Blue = steady sound / calm. The ad starts amber and ends blue.
Three motifs that tie every scene together (no random shots): (1) the ceiling — dark → filling with thoughts → packed at 1 AM → washed clean by the blue wave → morning sunlight; (2) the colors amber vs blue; (3) the clock — 11:04 PM, 12:03 AM, 1:07 AM on the bad night, 3:00 PM the next day, the same times again on the good night. Every shot is born from the previous one (ceiling → brain → ear → room), match cuts wherever possible.
Sound waves (3D, inside the ear): silence = flat line · phone recording = a wave that repeats with a visible seam, then cuts out · regular fan = jagged, wobbling wave · Spin360 = one smooth, even band that never changes.
Cast & set (same in every scene): the woman (~45, real-life look), her husband asleep beside her in some shots, one bedroom. Product: Spin360 always on the floor in the corner, white floor model only . 3:00 PM scenes must read clearly as daytime (bright window light, wall clock) so "three o'clock" never reads as 3 AM.
