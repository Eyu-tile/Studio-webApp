The emotional destination: “I want my bedroom to feel like that when I go to sleep.”

1 · The eight elements
1 — Desire
Surface desire: "I want my bedroom to feel better to sleep in."
Core Desire: Comfort.
________________


2 — Avatar
Primary: broad. Anyone who has ever cared how their bedroom feels at night. No demographic lock 
The only thing she needs to bring: she knows what white noise is. Not that she uses it — that she understands why people do. That's near-universal, and it costs us nothing at the top of the funnel.
Experiences / behaviors:
* Understands the idea that one steady background sound makes a room easier to sleep in. She may run it every night, she may have tried it once, she may have only heard people talk about it. All three qualify.
* Has one remembered night of a window cracked open, a porch, a breeze — air moving without being aimed at her.


Core Avatar Insight:
She already understands why steady sound makes a room easier to sleep in. Nobody ever applied that same idea to the air she's lying in.
________________


3 — Angle
Still air makes the bedroom feel less comfortable.
________________
4 — Mechanism
Spin360 pulls air in from above and below and sends it outward in 360°, and the unique blade shape gives the airflow a softer, more natural feel — a gentle breeze moving around the whole room instead of a stream hitting one spot.
The same blade produces the sound. A blade that pushes air rather than chopping through it makes a broader, steadier noise with no chop in it. One mechanism, two outputs: the sound she already falls asleep to, and the movement she's never had.
________________


5 — Authority
In order of appearance: (1) white noise itself — the entire world already does this for the ears, which is borrowed authority with no seller behind it; (2) her own memory of the window night, which makes her the witness; (3) David Patrick, the inventor behind Shark Wheel, seen on Shark Tank, four years on this blade. No authority figure on screen — the ad earns authority by explaining.
________________


6 — Awareness
True Unaware. She doesn't know she has an air problem. The ad teaches it by borrowing a belief she already holds about her ears and moving it to her skin — white noise is the on-ramp, not her awareness level.


________________


7 — Emotion
Opens — curiosity: positive valence, mid intensity. A question about something she already does every night.
Mid — self-recognition: slightly negative, mid intensity. Her room is the half-solved room. Discomfort, not fear.
Ends — comfort: positive, mid intensity. The feeling from the window night, on purpose, every night.
________________


8 — Positioning
ENEMY
Still air in a closed room — and the two dials everyone reaches for that treat the wrong variable: the thermostat, which only changes a number, and the fan, which throws a hard stream at one spot.
FIRST AND ONLY
Not a quieter fan and not a better sound machine: the only thing that gives a room white noise you can hear and white noise you can feel, from one blade.
