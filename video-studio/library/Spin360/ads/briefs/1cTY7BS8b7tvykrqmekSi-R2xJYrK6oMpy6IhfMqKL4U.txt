# Element
	Answer
	Why this ad
	Spin360_CO2Bubble_HomeOffice runs about 1:40. This is the same idea in about 55 s, for people who won't sit through a long ad: the afternoon slump, the cause (UC Berkeley's "personal CO2 bubble"), why it matters (more CO2, lower decision-making scores), what breaks it up, and the Spin360. It also tests whether the bubble message works in a short format.
	What it's about
	Keep a clear head all afternoon: keep the air around you moving, without anything blowing in your face.
	How we make it
	Voiceover with captions, about 55 s, vertical 9:16. A new look, different from the long HomeOffice edit: a woman working in the corner of her small bedroom, door shut, and a "CO2 camera" view (the room in cool blue, her breath as an orange cloud) for the science part. Real product footage for the Spin360.
	Desire
	"I want to get through the afternoon with a clear head." (our wording, not a customer quote)
	Sub-avatar
	People who work from home in a small room with the door shut. By early afternoon they feel foggy, and they blame their sleep or their coffee.
	Angle
	The 2pm slump might be the air you're sitting in.
	Awareness
	They know the afternoon slump. They don't know about the CO2 bubble. The ad takes them from the problem to the cause to the product, fast.
	Belief
	"It's my sleep, it's my coffee." We move the blame to the still air around their face (H1 says it directly).
	Mechanism
	Berkeley: when you sit still, your own breath hangs in front of your face; gently moving air breaks it up. Another study: when office workers breathed more CO2, their decision-making scores dropped. Spin360: pulls air in from the top and bottom and circulates it around the whole office, nothing pointed at you.
	Authority / proof
	UC Berkeley (study 1, study 2): the bubble was there on all 41 people, at a desk, in a well-ventilated room; moving air broke it up. A second study (Harvard, not named in the VO): more CO2, lower decision-making scores. Then the inventor who reinvented the wheel on Shark Tank, right before the product claim.
	Emotion
	Recognition (foggy by 2pm) → curiosity (my own air?) → "so that's why" (CO2 and focus) → relief (it's fixable, gently) → the clear afternoon.
	Positioning
	Not air pointed at you. The air around your whole office keeps moving.
	Objections
	"My room is ventilated." → so was the room in the study. "I don't want air blowing on me all day." → nothing blowing your papers off the desk. "What if it doesn't work for me?" → 30 days to try it.
	Hooks
	3 spoken hooks for people who work from home, each from a different side: their belief (H1), their situation (H2), the proof (H3). Same body and CTA in all 3.
	Offer
	30 days to try it. No discount, no price.
	Production brief
Format: vertical 9:16, about 55 s. Voiceover with captions. It's short, so keep the cuts quick.
Voice: a woman in her 30s, warm and close, like a friend telling you something she just found out. She is the narrator, not the woman on screen talking. Natural pace, slowing down a little on "even in a well-ventilated room" and on the 8am line. The tone follows the ad: a little tired and relatable on the hook, curious on the science, more serious on why it matters, relief on "the good news", confident and simple on the Spin360, light on her day (you can hear the smile), and calm on the CTA, with no urgency.
The CO2 camera: this is the signature visual of the ad. When we talk about the science, the shot switches to a sensor-camera look: the room goes cool blue and her breath shows up as a soft orange cloud that just sits in front of her face (it doesn't stream out of her mouth). Use the same look every time, so people recognize it. When the air starts moving, light-blue streams come in and the orange breaks up.
Captions: white, a few words at a time. Each hook has its own short on-screen line (in the table).
Footage you can pull from: our UGC library (the product in real homes) and past ads with David and the Shark Tank moment. Don't reuse shots from the long HomeOffice edit: this one should look new.
