Script: 
A hundred years ago, mothers were hanging their babies outside apartment windows. In cages. Five stories up.
And in Sweden, parents still put their babies outside to sleep in the snow. Walk past a café — row of strollers out front, parents inside drinking coffee.
And a hundred years ago, the President slept on the roof of the White House. Taft had a porch built up there. The whole country had sleeping porches.
So. Baby cages. Babies napping in the freezing cold. Presidents sleeping on rooftops.
They had one thing in common. And that's what got me.
I'm David Patrick. Ten years ago I invented a square wheel, took it on Shark Tank — and then spent four years trying to get that feeling, being outside with that natural air moving around you, inside your house.
This is what came out of it. It's called Spin360.
It feels like somebody took the walls off your house. You've got this breeze moving across your face, your arms, the whole room. Gentle. Constant. You lie down at night, close your eyes, and you'd swear your bed was out in the yard — except it's the temperature of your house. You wake up, and the room still feels open.
And the way it works is actually simple. It pulls air from above and below and sends it out all the way around the room. But what matters is how it feels. You know when you grab a magazine and fan yourself? That big, easy push of air? I took that — and made it go in a circle.
People who have it tell me the same thing, every time: it feels like they're sitting outside. One customer wrote — "Take my bedroom outdoors."
Yep. That's the fan.
So if you want a house that feels like it's got no walls, go check it out. Put it in your bedroom for a week, and you'll see what I mean.




1 · The eight elements
1
Desire
	Surface desire: “I want my bedroom to feel better / more comfortable.”
Core Desire: Comfort. 


	2
Avatar
	Primary: U.S. women 45+, strongest signal in 55–65+.
Demographic evidence: Swedish Babies skewed heavily female, especially 45–64, with meaningful 65+. Sleeping Porch reinforced this even harder: 87% of spend went to women, concentrated primarily in 65+, 55–64 and 45–54; all 4 recorded results were women.
Experiences / behaviors:
* Strong affinity for open windows, porches, outdoor sleeping, breezes and moving air.
* Many already sleep with fans, open/cracked windows, or both, including in winter. Swedish comments repeatedly describe needing air movement to sleep.
* Often likes the contrast of being cozy/warm under blankets while cool air moves around the room.
* Some explicitly distinguish air movement from cooling — they want to feel the air move, not simply lower the temperature.
* Strong nostalgia / generational memory: grandparents’ houses, sleeping porches, childhood summers, open windows, attic fans, rain on roofs, nighttime sounds and outdoor smells.
* Sleeping Porch comments repeatedly describe these experiences as “best sleep ever,” “magical,” “wonderful,” and among their favorite childhood memories.
* She may want open-air sleeping but modern life gets in the way: crime/safety, humidity, bugs, noise and air quality.
* She may want moving air while disliking a concentrated fan blast directly on her face/body.
Psychographic: She cares a lot about how a room feels. She associates moving air with comfort, home, nature, nostalgia and better-feeling sleep environments. She isn't necessarily looking for “a stronger fan”; she wants to recreate the feeling of sleeping with the window open or on a porch, while still being comfortable and protected indoors.
Core Avatar Insight:
She doesn't just miss cooler air. She misses the feeling of sleeping with the world slightly open around her.




	3
Angle
	Still air makes the bedroom feel less comfortable.
	4
Mechanism
	Spin360 pulls air in from above and below and sends it outward in 360°, and the unique blade shape gives the airflow a softer, more natural feel — a gentle breeze moving around the whole room instead of a stream hitting one spot.
	5
Authority
	In order of appearance: (1) the historical sleeping porches themselves — America already built rooms for this feeling; (2) her own lived memory of both rooms, which makes her the witness; (3) two verbatim customer quotes; (4) David Patrick as the man who spent four years on it. No authority figure on screen — the ad earns authority by educating.
	6
Awareness
	True Unaware. She doesn’t know she has an air problem; the film teaches it through her own memory before the product exists.
	7
Emotion
	Opens — curiosity: positive valence, mid-high intensity. A strange true fact, watched for fun.
Ends — comfort: positive, mid intensity. Relief and warmth — the feeling can come back. Per the valence doctrine, the market lives in the fear quadrant; this ad sells from the opposite one.
	8
Positioning
	ENEMY
The sealed, still room — and the regular fan that answers it with a hard stream hitting the same spot.
FIRST AND ONLY
Not a stronger fan: a different way to move air through a room — softer, broader, more like a natural breeze.
