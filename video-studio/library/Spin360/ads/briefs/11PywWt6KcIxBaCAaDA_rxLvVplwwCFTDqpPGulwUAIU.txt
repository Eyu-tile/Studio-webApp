	Strategy
# Element
	Answer
	Desire
	"I want to sleep like I used to." He wants his bedroom to finally feel right at night: fresh air, no stuffiness, no fan blasting his face, so he and his wife sleep through and both wake up rested. Underneath: being the guy who found the smart fix for the house, not another gadget.
	Avatar
	Men 45–65, homeowners, buying for the bedroom they share with their wife (real buyers: "Got my wife one. She loves it", "I have two… so perfect in the bedroom"). They like knowing how things work and are skeptical of gadgets and price ("pricey, but… worth it"). Behavior: they keep throwing fans at the problem (a box fan pointed at the bed, the ceiling fan, the AC, even "four fans surrounding our bed") and the room still feels stuffy or uneven. Belief to respect: "a fan is a fan, it just blows air at you."
	Angle
	The same shape that reinvented the wheel makes the whole room one temperature with a real breeze, so the bedroom feels like sleeping outside, at the temperature of your own home. One reason to buy: smarter engineering, not more air.
	Mechanism
	New mechanism (patented): every fan pulls air from the back and blows it out the front; this shape pulls from the top and the bottom and pushes it out 360°. That mixes the ceiling air and the floor air into one temperature and turns airflow into a gentle breeze instead of a blast. We sell the feeling (outdoors, indoors); the shape is the reason to believe it.
	Authority
	Title + experience: the founder who reinvented the wheel and made a deal with Mark Cuban on Shark Tank, using the same shape he has had for 20 years. Plus the patent. Shown before any pitch.
	Awareness
	Hook: Unaware (pattern interrupt, no product). Body takes him from "my bedroom just feels off" (Problem Aware) to "a fan built differently fixes it" (Solution Aware). The fan market is sophisticated (stage 3–4), so his objection is "how is yours different?" and the shape and the mechanism carry the ad.
	Emotion
	Curiosity (what is this guy doing?) → "that's clever" (he feels like the smart buyer who gets the engineering) → relief and calm (the bedroom feels like outside).
	Positioning
	Different, not "better": not a fan that blows in your face or a ceiling fan pushing hot air down, but a breeze maker for the whole room. The enemy is the fan aimed at you.
	Format
	Founder string-out modeled on our winning David ad (reference below): real story → how it works → benefits. Hook in full screen; then split screen in the beginning (bridge and story) and full screen B-roll from about the middle on (benefits), like the winner.
	Hooks
	3 visual hooks of the drill prototype, same action, different places. Same body in all three.
	Offer
	Risk reversal: 30 nights to try it. If you don't love it, send it back. No fake scarcity.

Bring David back on screen when it helps. Your call.
