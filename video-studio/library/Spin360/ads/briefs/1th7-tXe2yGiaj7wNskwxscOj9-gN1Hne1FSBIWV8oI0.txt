	Strategy
# Element
	Answer
	Desire
	"I want to sleep like I used to." Fresh, moving air at night, like sleeping outside, so they wake up rested instead of stuffy and tired, with no fan blasting them in the face.
	Avatar
	Homeowners 45–65 sharing a closed bedroom. Curious about how things work (they stop for the guy with the drill) and skeptical of gadgets. Behavior: they already tried a box fan, the ceiling fan, the AC, and the room still feels stuffy. Belief to respect: "a fan is a fan, it just blows air at you."
	Angle
	One reason to buy: the guy who reinvented the wheel turned "the worst propeller ever" into something that pulls air from the floor and the ceiling at the same time and mixes the whole room, so the first night feels like sleeping outside.
	Mechanism
	A regular propeller sucks air in one way and sends it right back out. This shape sucks air in from both directions (the coldest air at the floor and the hottest air at the ceiling) and sends it out like Saturn's rings, so the breeze hits your whole body. The hook shows it: the smoke gets pulled in.
	Proof
	The smoke pulled in on camera, David's first night ("I thought I was outside"), his friends' texts ("best night's sleep ever"), two real customers, and the interviewer refusing to give hers back.
	Authority
	David Patrick, the inventor who reinvented the wheel. He says it himself in the first line, before any product talk.
	Awareness
	Hook: Unaware (what is this guy doing?). The body takes them from "my bedroom just feels stuffy" (Problem Aware) to "this fan mixes the whole room like outside" (Product Aware). Sophisticated market: the shape and the smoke answer "how is yours different?".
	Emotion
	Curiosity (the smoke), a laugh (the boat), "oh, that's how it works", wonder (the walls are gone), trust (real people), then want (he can't live without it).
	Format
	Drill hook, then a real interview string-out of David (no new voice-over). Visual idea for this one: the air you can see. Every time he talks about air, we show the air (smoke, mist, arrows).
	Hooks
	3 drill hooks in new real public places with smoke already in the scene, filmed like a stranger caught it on their phone: an Austin BBQ line, an LA night market, a Little Havana domino table. Same on-screen text on all three.
	Offer
	No discount and no sales line in the voice. End card only: "Try it for 30 nights."
