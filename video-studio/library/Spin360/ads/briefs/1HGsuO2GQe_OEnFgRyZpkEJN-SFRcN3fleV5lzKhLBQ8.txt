# Element
	Answer
	1 Desire
	A baby who finally sleeps through the night, and a mom who gets her nights, her energy and herself back, instead of just surviving the first year.
Core desire: comfort (rest, peace of mind) + health (a baby who grows up healthy).
	2 Avatar
	Experiences: up every two hours for weeks or months; tried the swaddles, the sound machine, the blackout curtains and the sleep schedule, and nothing changed.
Behaviors: googles baby sleep at 2 a.m., buys sleep gadgets, checks the monitor all night, follows sleep accounts on Instagram and TikTok.
Emotions: exhausted, frustrated, quietly guilty ('what am I doing wrong?'), loves her baby but is running on empty.
Demographic: US moms, 25 to 38, baby 0 to 12 months, first or second baby.
	3 Angle
	Sealed nursery air keeps the baby waking every 2 hours
	4 Mechanism
	Problem: with the door and the window closed, the air in the nursery stops moving; every exhale (used-up air, CO₂) stays around the baby's face and gets breathed back in, so the baby can't settle and wakes up.
Solution: Spin360 pulls air in from the top and the bottom and sends it out all the way around (360°), so the air around the crib keeps moving like outside; the used-up air drifts away, every breath is fresh air, with a soft, gentle breeze and no noise.
	5 Authority
	In order of appearance: 1) the Nordic tradition: parents in Norway, Sweden and Finland nap their babies outside, even in the snow; 2) research on outdoor naps: those babies sleep almost twice as long; 3) simple breathing science: every breath out is CO₂; 4) real proof: one mom's first baby never slept more than two hours at a time, her second slept five with Spin360.
The ad earns authority by educating: no expert or doctor on screen.
	6 Awareness
	Problem-aware: she knows her baby wakes up every two hours (the hook calls it out), but she has no idea the cause is the still air in the nursery, and she has never heard of a solution like this.
Strange fact: in Norway, Sweden and Finland, parents put their babies outside to sleep in the snow, and those babies sleep almost twice as long..
	7 Emotion
	  

Opens: curiosity and surprise (positive, medium-high): 'wait, babies sleep outside in the snow?'
Mid: worry and realization (negative, high): the used-up air around her baby's face, the 2 a.m. crying, 'so that's why'.
Ends: relief and peace (strongly positive, high): the baby asleep, arms up, and she finally sleeps and feels like herself again.
	8 Positioning
	Enemy: the still, sealed air in the nursery, and the gadgets that never touch it (the AC only cools, the purifier only cleans, a regular fan blasts the baby).
First and only: an air circulator made for babies' rooms that keeps the air around the crib gently moving, like outside, without ever blowing on the baby.
