Who buys this ad: the same buyer as the original Swedish Babies VSL that's scaling — a woman, Unaware, who sleeps badly. The babies are the PROOF; the promise is HER sleep.


What this ad is: the original Swedish Babies ad, same beat order and same claims — with one change: the hook is now a short LIVE SCENE (~20s of acting) instead of narrated stock footage. The scene ends on the question "But... why?" — and the VO answers it with a documentary opening: "This is Finland." The whole ad is the answer to the question the character asks.


Why the scene is short: this is a feed hook, not theater. The absurd image is the FIRST FRAME — a viewer scrolling sees babies sleeping in the snow within 3 seconds. The dialogue doesn't build up to the shock; it deepens it (confirms it's intentional, scales it from one mother to a whole culture).




The documentary touch: the VO opens by planting the flag — "This is Finland" — short, dry, factual sentences, exactly like the original's narrator. The research behind the outdoor-napping claim is Finnish (University of Oulu), so Finland strengthens the claim.






* The mothers are always nearby — visible through the window with coffee, calm, keeping an eye. Present, just their way.
* The local woman is never weird — she's the one who knows. The visitor asks the viewer's questions.


THE CAST (locked — copy verbatim into every generation): THE VISITOR: woman, mid-30s, American. Shoulder-length dark blonde hair, winter coat, scarf. Expressive face — reacts big but real. THE LOCAL (Finnish): woman, mid-30s. Light blonde hair in a low bun, wool sweater or winter coat depending on scene, calm in everything she does. THE BABIES: under 1 year, bundled in warm sleep sacks inside classic prams with canopies (lead pram: dark green). Always asleep, warm, visible.


THE LOCATIONS: Hook A + C: a cozy café in a small Finnish town — big front window facing the street; through it, the sidewalk where prams park. Warm lamps inside, blue winter light and gentle snow outside. Hook B: the snow-dusted front porch of a warm wooden house.




The storyboard is the plan, not a cage. Locked: the honesty rule · the original's beat order · the "But... why?" ending on every hook · mothers visible and calm · the 2s of real fan audio · the final mirror. Yours: angles, timing inside beats, café/porch details.




2. THE HOOKS — 3 SCENES (~20s each · same ending line · pick per variant)
HOOK A — "STROLLER ROW" (street walk-up · cultural scale)
[The two women walk up the snowy sidewalk. The frame reveals: a ROW of prams parked in the snow, babies asleep inside, snow falling on the canopies.]


VISITOR (stopping cold): Wait — why are all these babies out here? It's freezing.


LOCAL (casual, barely slowing down, heading for the café): They're napping. They sleep better outside — they all do. (nods at the window) The moms are inside.


[Through the window: mothers with coffee, calm, keeping an eye.]


VISITOR (still staring at the row): But there's snow. Actual snow.


LOCAL (small smile, opening the café door): Babies here have slept outside for a hundred years. It's the deepest sleep they get.


VISITOR (one last look at the row): But... why?


[The LOCAL just smiles. HOLD 1s. CUT — VO in.]
HOOK B — "THE PORCH" (home visit · family tradition)
[FIRST FRAME: a pram on a snow-dusted porch, baby asleep inside, snow falling. The VISITOR arrives at the front door with the LOCAL.]


VISITOR (stopping): Is that— your baby is sleeping out here?


LOCAL (opening the door, warm): Best place for her nap. She sleeps twice as long out here.


VISITOR: In the snow?


LOCAL: She's warm — look at her. (beat) My mother did the same with me.


VISITOR (looking at the sleeping baby, snow settling on the canopy): But... why does it work?


[The LOCAL smiles, walks inside. HOLD on the baby, 1s. CUT — VO in.]
HOOK C — "THROUGH THE WINDOW" (café interior · witness shock)
[FIRST FRAME: inside the café, warm. Through the window: a MOTHER parks her pram next to TWO others in the snow, tucks the blanket, and walks toward the door — alone.]


VISITOR (mid-sip, to her friend): Wait. She's just... leaving him out there?


LOCAL (not even looking up from her coffee): Mm-hm. They nap better outside. All of them do.


[The VISITOR looks again — the row of prams, snow falling.]


VISITOR: There's snow. Actual snow.


LOCAL (small smile): Babies here have slept outside for a hundred years. Deepest sleep they get.


VISITOR (staring out the window): But... why?


[HOLD 1s. CUT — VO in.]
