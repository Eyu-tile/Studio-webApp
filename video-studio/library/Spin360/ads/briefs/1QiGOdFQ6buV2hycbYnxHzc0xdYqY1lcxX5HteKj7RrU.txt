CONTENT reference — this is what we're showing: Chris Hadfield "Sleeping in Space" — real ISS sleep pod footage, astronaut bedtime routine, the actual environment we're recreating. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UyFYgeE32f0
INSTRUCTIONS TO COPY AND PASTE INTO YOUR AI CHAT  FOR YOUR GENERATION:
All AI-generated images and footage for this ad must be photorealistic and cinematic. If it looks like AI, it doesn't work. The standard is: if someone sees it in their feed, they think a production company spent serious money filming this. It should feel like both a high-budget film and a real documentary at the same time — so polished it looks expensive, so realistic it looks true. That combination is what makes people stop and pay attention. Push for maximum realism — real skin texture, natural lighting, no plastic look, no AI glow.
Cinema, not PowerPoint. Don't just generate one completely different  image per sentence. Build continuous environments where the story lives. Same space, Same characters, multiple angles, multiple cuts. The environment narrates — not the other way around. If you remove the audio and the viewer still understands what's happening, that's cinema.
Kuleshov: astronaut's face → CO2 cloud forming → back to face with discomfort expression. The viewer connects cause and effect without needing text to explain it.
Two visual worlds with their own color. ISS = cold blue, harsh artificial light, claustrophobic. Earth/nature = warm golden, soft light, open. The contrast between the two tells the story before the audio does.
The ISS → Earth transition needs to be a MOMENT. Not a hard cut. Slow dissolve or match cut — like a close-up of the astronaut's face in the sleep pod dissolves into the woman's face sleeping in her bedroom. Same framing, two worlds.

[4] Script

Hooks: 
The most advanced bedroom ever built is floating 250 miles above Earth. And the people inside it can't sleep.
There's a bedroom that cost 150 billion dollars to build. And the people sleeping in it still can't get through the night.  
The most advanced machine ever built still can't solve one basic human problem. Sleep. 
Body:
The most advanced bedroom ever built is floating 250 miles above Earth. And the people inside it can't sleep.


So on the ISS astronauts kept waking up with headaches. Every morning. Foggy, exhausted. Sleeping pills became the second most used drug up there. They tried everything and nothing worked.
Then one sleep researcher figured it out. In zero gravity air doesn't move. So the CO2 you breathe out just sits there. Right in front of your face. You're literally sleeping in a little cloud of your own breath all night.
So NASA puts a tiny fan in every sleep pod. Just enough to move air across their face. A little breeze. And that was it. Headaches gone. Sleep got way deeper.
Now here's where it gets interesting. Back on Earth gravity takes care of the CO2 thing sure. But the air in your bedroom barely moves. Like at all. All night. And your body wasn't designed for that. We were built to sleep outside. In forests. On beaches. Places where the air is always doing something. That's when we sleep the deepest. Always have.
So someone spent four years trying to figure out how to recreate that. And what they came up with is kind of nuts. It pulls air in from above and below at the same time mixes everything together and pushes it out in every direction. Like Saturn's rings. Same temperature everywhere. Air always moving always fresh. It doesn't blast you it just moves around you.
And when you feel it — okay this is the part that gets people — it feels like the walls of your room just aren't there anymore. Like your bed is on a beach in Hawaii and there's this perfect breeze coming off the water. You close your eyes and you don't even mean to fall asleep but you just do. And you wake up actually rested. Once you feel it you're kind of hooked honestly.
Below speed five? Dead silent. Doesn't even show up on a decibel meter. Above five it makes this really beautiful white noise. Not like a regular fan. More like this natural shhhh that just makes everything else disappear. It was made for sleep. That was the whole point.
Then there's the scent pods which honestly might be my favorite part. You drop one in and like ten seconds later your whole room smells like a forest. Pine cedar eucalyptus. Put citronella in? Bugs gone. It's basically a scent system that happens to also be a fan.
It was made by the guy who invented the Shark Wheel. Like literally reinvented the wheel. Went on Shark Tank got a deal with Mark Cuban Kevin O'Leary and Nick Woodman. And now he made this. First thousand people get a hundred bucks off. Honestly just go try it. You're gonna be obsessed.
