	4.  Strategic breakdown
Why each section exists and what it is meant to make her feel.
HOOK  (0:00–0:08)  ·  Surprised — positive, high intensity
A strange fact with no product and no category in sight. The fan never appears in the hook — hard rule. The viewer has to form her own question, which is why the hook is a statement, not a question.
STORY  (0:08–0:40)  ·  curiosity holds
Third-person documentary, Netflix retelling. The contrast does the work: they had everything, and they slept outside. Then it widens — grandmas, kids, babies, doctors — so this stops being a curiosity about one building and becomes something everyone used to do. “We stopped. And forgot why we ever started” puts the viewer inside the loss with the word we.
THE KNIFE  (0:40–1:00)  ·  Afraid — negative, high intensity
The consequence arrives before the cause: anxiety, depression, the researchers pointing inside. Then it lands on her — eight hours a night, breathing the same dead air. The deepest cut is the self-blame: she blamed stress, age, herself. Then the betrayal: the powerful families protected themselves and nobody told her.
BRIDGE  (1:00–1:15)  ·  the turn
“I know why. And one guy in California knows why.” The narrator and the inventor both hold the answer she doesn't. The reveal corrects the wrong conclusion — it was never the porch, it was the air — which is what allows the product to exist indoors.
PRODUCT  (1:15–2:35)  ·  climbing to Relaxed
Mechanism first, then sensation with no interruption: the double-take, the walls disappearing, the shoulders dropping. The name and the patent land AFTER she has felt it — confirmation, not spec sheet. Benefit stack: silence, scent, daily use. The night payoff answers the knife in the same words it hurt — tired becomes rested, foggy becomes clear.
CLOSE  (2:35–end)  ·  Relaxed — positive, low intensity
Authority, social proof, then “Those families knew this. Now you do too” — which pays off the betrayal. Price anchor, soft CTA, no urgency spike. The ad must end in the state the product sells: calm.
5. Edit brief 


Item
	Direction
	Style
	Slow mini-documentary — History Channel / National Geographic. Era photos with slow push-ins. No fast cuts. Audience is women 45–70: slower, simpler to follow, easy on the eye.
	Footage
	Real archive material over AI generation. Period photos of White House and American sleeping porches exist — use them. AI only as last resort, never for human moments. 
	Voice
	Calm American female documentary narrator. Conversational pace (~190 wpm), slowing on the knife, the night payoff and the close. Never salesy — if it starts sounding like an ad, pull back to just talking.
	Sound
	Quiet bed under the story. The knife section can go sparse. Let the close breathe.
