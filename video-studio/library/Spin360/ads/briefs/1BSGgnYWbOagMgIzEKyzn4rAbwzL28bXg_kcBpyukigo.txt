Brief - Spin360_CO2Bubble_Sleep.mp4
3 ads, one edit, about 85 s. Voiceover with captions. Only the hook changes; the body and CTA are the same in all 3. The images are inspiration for the idea of each shot, not a storyboard.
	

The idea: you can sleep eight hours and still wake up tired. Science found a cloud of your own breath that sits around your face when the air is still. The Spin360 keeps the air around your bed gently moving all night, so you wake up rested.
	Strategy
# Element
	Answer
	Why this ad
	Lots of people sleep eight hours and still wake up tired, and they blame stress or coffee. UC Berkeley found a "personal CO2 cloud" around every person they tested, and moving air broke it up. That gives them a new reason to look at the air in their bedroom, and the Spin360 is built to keep that air gently moving.
	What it's about
	Wake up rested: keep the air around you moving all night, without a fan blowing cold air in your face.
	How we make it
	Voiceover with captions, about 85 s, vertical 9:16. A simple animated cloud over the face for the science part, real product footage for the Spin360, and generated bedroom shots where we don't have footage.
	Desire
	"I want to wake up and actually feel rested." (our wording, not a customer quote)
	Sub-avatar
	Adults who sleep eight hours and still wake up exhausted. They sleep with the door and window shut, and blame their stress, their coffee or their bedtime.
	Angle
	The reason you wake up tired might be the air right in front of your face.
	Awareness
	They know they wake up tired. They don't know about the CO2 cloud. The ad takes them from the problem to the cause to the product.
	Belief
	"It's my stress, my coffee, my age." We move the blame to the air (H2 says it directly). Second belief: "a fan in winter = cold air on me", handled before the product.
	Mechanism
	Berkeley: when the air around you sits still, a cloud of your own breath stays around your face; moving air breaks it up. Spin360: pulls air in from the top and bottom and gently circulates it 360° around the room, with no blast at your pillow.
	Authority / proof
	UC Berkeley (study 1, study 2): the cloud was there on all 41 people; a small fan broke most of it up. Plus David Patrick, the inventor who reinvented the wheel on Shark Tank. We say openly that the study was done at desks, not in beds.
	Emotion
	Recognition (tired after 8 hours) → curiosity (a cloud on my face?) → relief (the air keeps moving, no cold blast) → the rested morning.
	Positioning
	Not a fan that blows at you. The air around your whole bed keeps moving.
	Objections
	"Is this proven in sleep?" → we say the study was at desks, not in beds. "A fan will make me cold" → no blast at your pillow. "What if it doesn't work for me?" → 30 nights to try it.
	Hooks
	3 spoken hooks that call out people who wake up tired, each from a different side: the hidden cause (H1), their belief (H2), a hidden fear (H3). Same body and CTA in all 3.
	Offer
	30 nights to try it. No discount, no price.
	Production brief
Format: vertical 9:16, about 85 s. Voiceover with captions.
Voice: calm and warm, like someone telling a friend about something they found out.
The cloud: one simple visual for the "personal CO2 cloud": a soft, warm-colored haze in front of the face. Use the same look every time it appears, so people recognize it.
Captions: white, a few words at a time. Each hook has its own short on-screen line (in the table).
Footage you can pull from: our UGC library (the product in real bedrooms) and past ads with David and the Shark Tank moment.

	1 · The cloud
	It's called a personal CO2 cloud. Researchers at UC Berkeley measured the air around 41 people sitting at their desks. Every single one of them had more CO2 around their face than the rest of the room. That's your own breath, just hanging there.
	  

  

  

	Someone at a desk breathing out, and a soft cloud stays in front of their face. Show the real study for a second and put "UC Berkeley · 41 out of 41" on screen.
	2 · Moving air
	And when they used a small fan to move the air, most of that cloud just broke up.
	  

	The air starts moving lightly and the cloud breaks apart. Simple and clean.
	3 · Your bedroom
	Now, that study was done at desks, not in beds. But think about your bedroom. Door shut. Windows closed. You, lying in the same spot for eight hours.
	  

  

	Cut from the desk to the bedroom: the door shuts, the window is closed, someone lies still in bed for hours. Keep the honest line in the voice only, no text on screen for it.
	4 · Plastic bag / not a regular fan
	Without moving air, a closed room can feel like you're sleeping in a plastic bag. But you don't want a regular fan blasting cold air in your face all night either.
	  

  

	First half: the whole bed sealed inside a giant clear plastic bag, tied at the end like new-mattress packaging. She sleeps inside, the plastic a little fogged, stuffy. Plenty of space above her: the plastic never touches or covers her face. Second half: a regular fan blowing straight at her and she pulls the blanket up, cold. Neither one is what she wants.
	5 · Spin360 + David
	That's where the Spin360 comes in. It was created by David Patrick, the guy who reinvented the wheel on Shark Tank.
	  

  

	A second of the real Shark Tank moment, then the first clean look at the Spin360 next to a bed at night.
	6 · Mechanism
	Its patented design pulls air in from the top and bottom and gently circulates it around the entire room. A full 360 degrees. No strong blast pointed at your pillow.
	  

  

  

	Show the air being pulled in from the top and the bottom (smoke works well) and a soft ring of air going out around the whole room. The key word is gently.
	7 · All night
	So that cloud of used air doesn't just sit on your face. You turn off the light, pull up your blanket, and the air around you just keeps moving. All night.
	  

  

	The lamp goes off, the blanket goes up, and the room stays still and calm while the air quietly moves. The cloud never forms over her face.
	8 · The morning
	And then you wake up before the alarm. No second coffee needed. You actually feel rested. Your head is clear, not heavy. And the room feels fresh, not stuffy.
	  

  

	The opposite of the H2 morning: she is already awake and turns the alarm off before it rings, then stretches, rested, in a bright room. If you can, show the coffee untouched.
	9 · CTA
	Try it at home for 30 nights. If you don't love how you wake up, just send it back. Tap below and see how it works.
	  

	End card: "Try it for 30 nights".
