Brief - Spin360_RacingThoughts_NightTimeline.mp4


3 ads · same body · 3 visual hooks. A calm narrator walks through one night of an overthinker who sleeps next to the Spin360: minute one, minute ten, minute thirty, the next morning. Everything is shown as a glowing hologram: her body and her brain change color as she calms down.

Strategy
# Element
	Answer
	1 · Desire
	"I want to lie down and actually fall asleep, without my mind racing."
	2 · Avatar
	Women 40–60, tired but wired: exhausted all day, but the moment her head hits the pillow her mind starts racing. She checks the clock at 3 AM and starts the day already tired.
	3 · Angle
	Cool air calms the body, and the mind follows. One night, minute by minute.
	4 · Mechanism
	Your body has to cool down a couple degrees before you can fall asleep. Spin360 sends cool air out 360°, so it reaches your whole body from every side (a regular fan only hits your face). Then the deep, steady white noise slows the racing thoughts.
	5 · Proof
	A real customer: "I haven't slept this good in years." Plus 30 nights to try it.
	6 · Awareness
	Problem aware. She knows she can't turn her brain off at night. She doesn't know air can help.
	7 · Emotion
	Recognition in the hook ("that's me at 3 AM"), then calm, then relief in the morning.
	8 · Positioning
	Enemy: the hot, restless body that keeps the mind awake. Not a pill, not a white noise machine: air that calms your body first.
	Production brief
Voice: American narrator, calm and slow, like a sleep documentary. Warm, never dramatic. Spoken, not read.
Title on top, the whole video: What happens when an overthinker sleeps next to this (2 lines, white, bold, upper third).
Rounded frame: the picture sits in a rounded-corner frame in the center, black around it, like the reference.
Time markers: big on screen when the VO says them: MINUTE 1 · MINUTE 10 · MINUTE 30 · NEXT MORNING.
Captions: one word at a time, white, bold, center.
Color rule (keep it on every shot): orange = hot body or racing brain. Cyan = cool and calm. Brain agitation = fast flashing nodes; calm = slow, then still.
Sound: soft, deep, steady white noise under the whole ad, a bit louder at minute ten. 1 second of near silence after "Your mind goes quiet."
Rules: no discount, no season words, never say "silent".
Visual hooks (3 ads, 0:00–0:05)
Same voiceover on all three: "Here's what happens when an overthinker puts this 360 air circulator next to their bed." Only the picture changes. The hook must show that her brain is racing while her body lies still.
