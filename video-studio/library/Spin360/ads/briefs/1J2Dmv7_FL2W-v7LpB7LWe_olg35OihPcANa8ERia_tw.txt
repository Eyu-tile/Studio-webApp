Creative Direction — Spin360 / White Noise & Calm
The one-line idea
An ad with no voiceover. Music plus the real white-noise sound, and shots that show the problem and then the solution. We are repositioning the fan as a white-noise machine that also circulates a soft breeze — and that combination is what makes it right for sleep.
Hard rules
1. No voiceover. None. This is deliberate — the moment someone hears a person selling, they scroll. Without a voice it reads as organic; people watch and read instead of being pitched.
2. Deliver every variation twice: one cut with your own on-screen text, one cut with no text at all. The real goal is to sell it with no text — the text version is a fallback, and the copy will be rewritten anyway.
3. The viewer has to actually hear the white noise. Use the supplied audio file. Open on music to build the emotion in the problem section, then let the real white noise come through so they hear what the fan sounds like.
4. Do not open on the product. The competitor reel that shows the machine up front is not the reference — that is the ad we are not making.
5. Three variations, three different hooks. Same core idea, different way in.
Who we are selling to
People who live where it is never quiet — think New York City. Traffic, upstairs neighbors, a partner snoring, a dog barking, a train going past, hallway noise, and the random 3 AM sound that snaps your brain awake. Mostly problem aware: they know noise is wrecking their sleep, they have not necessarily decided white noise is the answer. Some are further along and already use apps, speakers, a fan, or a machine.
The promise: a room where their mind can settle into the sound and everything else disappears, because the noise is masked.
The emotional arc
The whole ad is one before/after. Before: annoyance, restlessness, frustration, no control over their own bedroom. After: calm, relief, ease — finally rested. Music carries the "before," the white noise carries the "after."
Competitive framing
The competitor (SNOOZ) started as a white-noise machine and built a fan around it. We are the same product from the other direction, with a better fan. Also worth landing against white-noise apps — theirs is a recording through a speaker, ours is a real sound made by a real blade.
Footage
Use the UGC shots — this concept lands better with them than with polished product footage, especially the sleeping set. It's fine to mix in other material; it doesn't have to be only sleeping shots. Showing David (the founder) is optional and allowed — a Shark Tank line is a valid option, not a requirement.
Tone references from the inspiration (the feel we want, not the shots to copy):
* "The goal is masking the unpredictable sounds that wake you up — snoring, traffic, neighbors, hotel hallways, the random 3 AM sound that makes your brain go 'hello'."
* The social clips about city noise: "it is silent in NYC right now, what is happening," the upstairs neighbor you hear through the ceiling, "do you wake up every time the train passes in the middle of the night?"
* The UGC line that captures the product: "you literally can't hear it, but when you turn it up there's this really soft gentle white noise… you have the gentle breeze around you and that steady little sound — everything is just there without being distracting."
Freedom
No shot list on purpose. Read the 8 Elements section above and understand what we are actually selling — every word there was chosen. Then build the ad the way you think it should be built. You will edit it better than I could plan it shot by shot.
Deliverables
* 3 variations, 3 different hooks
* Each one in 2 versions: with on-screen text / without on-screen text
* The supplied white-noise audio used somewhere in every cut
