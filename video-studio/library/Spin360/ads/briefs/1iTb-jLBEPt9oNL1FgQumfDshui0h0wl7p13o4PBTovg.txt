	Strategy
# Element
	Answer
	Desire
	"I want to sleep like I used to." He wants his bedroom to finally feel right at night: fresh air, no stuffiness, no fan blasting his face, so he and his wife sleep through and both wake up rested. Underneath: being the guy who found the smart fix for the house, not another gadget.
	Avatar
	Men 45–65, homeowners, buying for the bedroom they share with their wife (real buyers: "Got my wife one. She loves it", "I have two… so perfect in the bedroom"). They like knowing how things work and are skeptical of gadgets and price ("pricey, but… worth it"). Behavior: they keep throwing fans at the problem (a box fan pointed at the bed, the ceiling fan, the AC, even "four fans surrounding our bed") and the room still feels stuffy or uneven. Belief to respect: "a fan is a fan, it just blows air at you."
	Angle
	A real breeze in the bedroom without opening a window: close your eyes and it feels like the walls are gone, at the temperature of your own home.
	Mechanism
	New mechanism (patented): every fan pulls air from the back and blows it out the front; this shape pulls from the top and the bottom and blows it out 360°. That mixes the ceiling air and the floor air into one temperature and turns airflow into a gentle breeze across your whole body. We sell the feeling (no walls, outdoors indoors); the shape is the reason to believe it.
	Authority
	Title + experience: the founder who reinvented the wheel on Shark Tank, same shape, holds the patent. Personal experience: he sleeps with it every single night. Shown before the pitch.
	Awareness
	Hook: Unaware (pattern interrupt, no product). Body takes him from "my bedroom just feels off" (Problem Aware) to "a fan built differently fixes it" (Solution Aware). The fan market is sophisticated (stage 3–4), so his objection is "how is yours different?" and the shape and the mechanism carry the ad.
	Emotion
	Curiosity (what is this guy doing?) → "that's clever" (he feels like the smart buyer who gets the engineering) → wonder and calm (the walls disappear and he finally sleeps).
	Positioning
	Different, not "better": not a fan that blows in your face, but a breeze maker that makes the whole room feel like outdoors. Not about heat or cooling, about how the room feels. The enemy is the fan aimed at you.
	Format
	Interview / mini documentary. No split screen. David full screen on the couch (wide and close-up), and full-screen B-roll whenever he talks about something we can show.
	Hooks
	3 new drill hooks (coffee shop, lake at dawn, backyard hot tub). Same action as ShapeStory: the blade on the drill pulls in steam or mist that is already part of the place.
	Offer
	Risk reversal: 30 nights to try it. If it doesn't feel like outside, send it back. No fake scarcity.
