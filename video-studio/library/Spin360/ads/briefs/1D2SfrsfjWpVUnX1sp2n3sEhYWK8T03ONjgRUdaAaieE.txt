Brief - Spin360_HalfMillionWinterFan.mp4


3 ads, one edit, about 90 s. Voiceover with captions. Only the hook changes, and H3 skips the 2 bridge lines (rows 2–3). Everything else is the same in all 3. The images are inspiration for the idea of each shot, not a storyboard.
	

The idea: an inventor spent $469,000 on a fan made for sleeping in winter. For people who sleep with a fan, it finally makes sense: the air and the sound, without the cold blast.
	Strategy
# Element
	Answer
	Why this ad
	The founder story is our strongest authority, and the Evolve authority lesson says to show it before the pitch. A big, unexpected number ($469,000) plus a contradiction (a fan made for winter) makes people stop. Then we show why it makes sense for people who sleep with a fan.
	What it's about
	Why someone would spend $469,000 building a fan made for sleeping in winter: the air and the sound you love, without the cold blast, plus the warm air from the ceiling brought back down.
	How we make it
	Voiceover with captions, about 90 s, vertical 9:16. Real footage of the inventor and of the product in real bedrooms, plus generated shots where we don't have footage.
	Desire
	"I want the air and the sound all night, without cold air on my face." (our wording, not a customer quote)
	Sub-avatar
	People who sleep with a fan every night, even in winter, and end up turning it to the wall so it doesn't freeze them out.
	Angle
	Keep sleeping with a fan in winter, without the cold blast.
	Awareness
	Solution aware: they already use a fan. The ad shows why this one is different.
	Belief
	"A fan in winter sounds crazy." We agree first, then show why it makes sense for a fan sleeper.
	Mechanism
	It pulls air in from the top and bottom and gently circulates it around the room, instead of blowing at you. It also brings the warm air near the ceiling back down.
	Authority / proof
	The inventor who reinvented the wheel on Shark Tank, $469,000 invested, a patented design, and one real test: 78 at the ceiling, 71 at the floor, then 73 everywhere with the fan on.
	Emotion
	Curiosity (a winter fan?) → recognition (the fan pointed at the wall) → comfort (tucked in, drifting off).
	Positioning
	Different from the fan they already have: it doesn't need to be turned away.
	Objections
	"A fan in winter will make me cold" → it doesn't blow at you. "What if I don't like it?" → 30 nights to try it.
	Hooks
	3 spoken hooks. Hook + bridge change; everything from "That's why David created the Spin360" to the end is the same in all 3. H3 already calls out the fan sleeper, so it skips the bridge.
	Offer
	30 nights to try it. No discount, no price.
	Production brief
Format: vertical 9:16, about 90 s. Voiceover with captions.
Voice: warm and casual, like someone telling a friend about something they found. Not an announcer.
Captions: white, a few words at a time. "$469,000" big on screen in the hook and again at the close.
Footage you can pull from: our UGC library (the product in real bedrooms), the prototypes folder, and past ads with David and the Shark Tank moment (like this one). Generate what we don't have.
Sound: soft music under the voice. On row 10, let the real fan sound come through for a moment.
