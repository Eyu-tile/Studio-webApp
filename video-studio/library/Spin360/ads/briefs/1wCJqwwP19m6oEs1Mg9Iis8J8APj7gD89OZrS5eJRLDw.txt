1. THE IDEA
Who this ad is for: a woman scrolling her feed who sleeps badly — and has no idea a fan could have anything to do with it. She's not looking for a fan. She's not looking for anything. We have to stop her, hold her, and change how she thinks about the air in her bedroom — all in one video.


How the ad does it: David opens with an absurd claim — "I invented a fan that makes you sleep like a Swedish baby." That sentence creates two questions at once: he invented a WHAT? and what's the deal with Swedish babies? — and the only way to get both answers is to keep watching. The fan is announced in the first breath and then held back: it appears on screen for the first time at ~1:20, at the exact moment the story has made her beg to see it.


Why the body is a Q&A: every question David asks is the question she is asking herself at that exact second. Is it the cold? Is it something about those babies? What does this have to do with me? How do you get outside air inside? Isn't that just a fan? Does it actually work? What about the noise? He asks it, he answers it, and the next question is already loading. That rhythm is what holds her for three minutes — each answer opens the next gap.


The emotional arc: wonder (babies sleeping in the snow) → understanding (it's the air, not the cold) → recognition (MY bedroom is the sealed room) → curiosity (so what did he build?) → the reveal → belief (the blades, his own nights with it, the sound) → trust (the man has done this before).


What it must feel like: a guy on your feed telling you something wild he learned — never an ad. David is the inventor sharing his obsession, not a presenter selling a product. If a moment feels like a commercial, that moment is wrong.


The proof and the promise: the babies are the PROOF that moving air means deeper sleep. The promise is HER sleep. Every plate about the babies is working toward the plate of the woman waking up rested.
