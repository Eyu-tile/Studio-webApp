Brief - Spin360_FanAtTheWall.mp4


3 ads, one edit, ~32 s. Same VO body in all three; only the hook (first ~3 s) changes. Real footage first: pull from our UGC library (a scene list with timestamps will be added to this brief) plus a few simple real demos. Female VO.
	

The idea: people who sleep with a fan turn it to face the wall so the blast doesn't hit them. This ad tells them why they have to, and shows the fan that doesn't need it. The wall is the visual thread: the regular fan always faces the wall; the Spin360 never does.
	Strategy
# Element
	Answer
	Desire
	Keep the fan on all night (for the moving air and the sound) without a blast hitting you. In their words: "I point it at the wall across the room from me so it doesn't blow on me, I just need the sound", "In the winter I turn it to low and have it face the wall so I don't get cold", "I can't sleep without a fan but unless it's hot, I don't like it pointed at me", "circulation without the cold blast".
	Avatar
	Adults 30–65 who sleep with a fan year-round and turn it to face the wall, especially in winter, because the direct blast makes them cold or dries them out. They keep it on for the moving air and the steady sound. Often a couple where one person needs the fan and the other hates feeling it.
	Angle
	One idea only: the fan that doesn't need to be pointed at the wall. Every line serves that idea. The wall opens the ad and closes it.
	Mechanism
	A regular fan's blades are thin like a knife: they cut into the air and twist it into one strong stream (a vortex) aimed at one spot. The Spin360 blade doesn't cut, it pushes with its whole flat face, like fanning yourself with a book, but all the way around (360°). Only the part of the blade facing you pushes air your way, so only a little reaches you. You still get moving air and the sound, just no blast.
	Proof
	Shown, not said: (1) a tissue or ribbon in front of a regular fan whips hard; in front of the Spin360 it only sways; (2) ribbons tied around the Spin360 all flutter outward at once (full circle); (3) hair still on the pillow. Authority at the end: Shark Tank.
	Authority
	"It's from the guy who reinvented the wheel on Shark Tank" (Shark Wheel). Show the wheel and the Shark Tank footage on that line so nobody hears "reinvented the wheel" as a figure of speech.
	Awareness
	Problem aware: they already live with the blast and already have their own fix (the wall). The ad names their fix, explains why they need it, and removes the need.
	Positioning
	Not "a better fan". The fan you don't turn to the wall. Hook 3 adds the category: the first fan you don't turn to the wall in winter. Never say it heats or cools anything.
	Emotion
	Recognition (that's my fan) → "oh, that's why" → relief (keep the air and the sound, lose the blast) → calm (fan right next to the bed) → trust (Shark Tank + 30 nights).
	Format
	Voiceover with b-roll, ~32 s, vertical 9:16. Real footage first: UGC shots from our library + real product demos. Warm, natural female VO, like a friend telling you something useful, not an announcer. Low music. Captions on every line so it works with sound off.
	Hooks
	3 hooks, same body. Each hook goes straight into the first body line, no bridge. The first frame must already show a regular fan facing a wall.
	Offer
	30 nights to try it. If you'd rather go back to the wall, send it back. No discount, no price on screen.
