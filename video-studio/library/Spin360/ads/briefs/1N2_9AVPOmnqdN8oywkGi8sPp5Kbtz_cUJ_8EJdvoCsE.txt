2. SCRIPT 
STORY
A hundred years ago, American children went to school outside. Rain, snow, or shine.
Nobody thought it was strange. Desks in the grass. Cots on the roof. That was just school. Hundreds of them, in a hundred and thirty cities.
And it worked. The children got healthier. They did better in their lessons.
Then, in the nineteen forties, it stopped. One by one, the schools closed — and the whole thing disappeared, like it had never been there.
And nobody ever stopped to ask what all that time outside had been doing for those children.
The answer has everything to do with you.
Every night of your life, you've slept sealed in. Windows shut. Door closed. Eight hours, all year round. That's the experiment. And our bodies never adapted to it.
One inventor in California finally asked the question. What was all that time outside actually doing? He spent four years chasing the answer.
And what he came up with is honestly hard to explain until you feel it.
PRODUCT
Here's what happens. It pulls air in from above and below at the same time, and because of the way the blade is designed, you feel this soft breeze showing up around different parts of the room. Across your face, then your arms, then somewhere else a moment later. Then there's this gentle movement of air throughout the whole room.
And after a few minutes, you almost forget the thing is on. You just notice the room feels better. More open. More comfortable. You sit back, relax a little, and pretty soon you just want to stay there.
And that's when the answer to those old outdoor schools starts to make sense.
Outside, air never really sits still. It's always moving around you, from different directions, even when you barely notice it. Those children spent their entire day surrounded by that.
That's what David spent four years trying to recreate indoors.
He called it Spin360.
And people end up leaving it on while they're reading, working, sitting on the couch, or winding down at night. They don't leave it on just because they're hot. They leave it on because they like the way the room feels.
At night, you notice it even more. You get into bed and there's this soft movement of air around you. After a while, you stop paying attention to where it's coming from. You just feel comfortable.
It's the same reason a room can feel so good when you crack a window before bed. The air is moving, the room doesn't feel so closed, and you settle in.
David Patrick is the inventor behind Spin360 — the same inventor who created the Shark Wheel and appeared on Shark Tank.
Thirty-five thousand Spin360s sold in the first six months.
Right now it's $189, before the price moves to $299.
And if you've ever opened a window simply because the room felt better that way, you'll understand Spin360 the second you feel it.
Go check it out.
