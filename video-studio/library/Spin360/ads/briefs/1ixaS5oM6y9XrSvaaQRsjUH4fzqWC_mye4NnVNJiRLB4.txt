SPIN360 — AC / ONE ROOM, TWO TEMPERATURES
EDITING DIRECTION + STRATEGIC BREAKDOWN
THIS IS DIRECTION, NOT A SHOT-BY-SHOT LOCK.
Understand the story, the belief we are building, and the visual role of each section. Then use the strongest footage available. The story order is intentional; the visual interpretation is flexible.
	1. CORE EDITING IDEA
The video should feel like a short documentary that slowly reveals a simple idea: one room can hold noticeably different temperatures at the same time. We first make the viewer curious, then show the behavior that follows from that problem, then let David explain it, and only after that reveal Spin360 as the logical solution.
The edit should never feel like we are rushing to sell a fan. The viewer should feel like they learned something useful about their own home before the product becomes the answer.
2. STRATEGIC BREAKDOWN
This is the logic the visuals need to protect. If a shot is visually attractive but works against this logic, do not use it.
ELEMENT
	WHAT THE EDIT SHOULD COMMUNICATE
	DESIRE
	Feel comfortable without constantly lowering the thermostat; get more value from the cold air the AC is already making.
	AVATAR
	Broad top-of-funnel AC user/homeowner. The recognizable behavior is more important than demographics: cold feet, a warmer bed/couch area, and repeatedly lowering the thermostat.
	ANGLE
	The same room can be 78°F and 71°F at the same time.
	MECHANISM
	Warm air rises, cold air sinks, and Spin360 helps mix air from different levels of the room.
	AUTHORITY
	David Patrick enters as the inventor and as the person explaining/testifying to the temperature-mixing test.
	AWARENESS
	Start broad/unaware. We should not open with a fan, a product feature, or “save money on AC.” Let the viewer discover why the problem matters.
	EMOTIONAL ARC
	Curiosity → recognition/frustration → explanation → proof → relief/control.
	POSITIONING
	Your AC makes the cold air. Spin360 helps move that air through the room — where people actually live.
	3. EDITING LANGUAGE
Think mini-documentary, not traditional performance ad.
Clean, curious, easy to follow. The story and the information should create retention; the edit should support them rather than overwhelm them.
Audience-first pacing.
Do not cut like a hyperactive TikTok edit. Use clear visual ideas, readable information, and enough screen time for an older/broader audience to understand what they are seeing.
Do not illustrate every sentence literally.
One strong visual can carry several lines. Let David stay on screen when his presence is doing the work. Use B-roll when it adds understanding, proof, or a new visual beat.
Keep visual and spoken hooks congruent.
If the VO is asking how one room can be 78°F and 71°F, the first visual should immediately communicate “two temperatures / one room,” not generic summer footage.
Delay the product until the story earns it.
There is no arbitrary second where Spin360 must appear. The reveal should happen when the viewer naturally asks: “So how do I move the cold air I already have?”
Use real material whenever possible.
Real David, real Spin360 footage, real reviews, and existing motion graphics should carry the ad. AI should be a last-resort connective tool, not the foundation of the edit.
4. VISUAL SOURCE PRIORITY
Use this hierarchy when choosing footage:
1 — REAL DAVID INTERVIEW FOOTAGE: Primary authority and engagement. Let the viewer see him speaking, not just hear him under B-roll.
2 — REAL SPIN360 PRODUCT / LIFESTYLE FOOTAGE: Whenever the product is clearly recognizable, prefer the real product. The exact shot does not need to match every sentence.
3 — EXISTING AFTER EFFECTS / MOTION GRAPHICS: Especially the top-and-bottom intake and outward 360° airflow explanation, temperature labels, gauges, arrows, and simple room-layer diagrams.
4 — REAL-WORLD SUPPORTING B-ROLL: Thermostat, AC vent, bedroom/living room, cold feet/warm upper body behavior, compressor, bills/review screenshots, etc. Use what feels native and believable.
5 — AI-GENERATED SHOTS: Use sparingly and only when a connective visual is genuinely missing. If an AI shot looks artificial, skip it. Do not rely on AI to show Spin360, David, the test, reviews, or proof.
________________


5. THREE VISUAL HOOK VARIATIONS
All three hooks can use the same opening audio. Build one shared body edit after the hook. These are visual directions, not mandatory shot lists.
VARIATION
	CORE VISUAL
	EXECUTION IDEAS
	OPTIONAL TOS
	HOOK 1
TWO READINGS, ONE ROOM
	Open immediately on 78°F and 71°F existing in the same room.
	Best version: two real digital thermometers/gauges at different heights. If that footage does not exist, build the same idea with clean After Effects labels over a real interior. Make the contradiction readable in the first second. Avoid fake “scientific” visuals.
	ONE ROOM.
TWO TEMPERATURES.
	HOOK 2

	Start on a thermostat showing one number, then reveal two different readings inside the same room.
	For example: thermostat → floor-level 71°F → higher-level 78°F. This can be real footage plus simple motion graphics. The point is that one thermostat reading does not visually describe the whole room.
	YOUR THERMOSTAT

	HOOK 3
COLD FEET / WARMER BODY
	Make the temperature difference human instead of technical.
	Open on a believable bedroom or couch moment: feet near the floor feel cold while the person still looks warm around the upper body. Add restrained 71°F and 78°F labels at different heights. Keep acting subtle; this should feel observed, not staged.
	71° DOWN LOW.
78° UP HIGH.
	6. BODY EDIT — STORY MAP
The sections below explain what each part of the story needs to accomplish. Use the visual ideas as a menu, not a checklist.
A. MYSTERY / DISCOVERY
Narrative job: make “one room, two temperatures” feel surprising but believable before we talk about a product.
* Real interior footage with simple temperature labels at different heights.
* Thermostat close-up as the “one number” contrast.
* Subtle room-layer motion graphic: warmer air higher, cooler air lower. Keep it clean and explanatory, not sci-fi.
* Slow push-ins, small reframes, or controlled camera movement are enough. The information is the hook.
B. HUMAN BEHAVIOR / THE THERMOSTAT LOOP
Narrative job: turn the abstract temperature difference into a behavior the viewer recognizes in themselves.
* Cold feet / warmer upper body in bed, on a couch, or in a chair.
* Thermostat changing 74 → 72 → 70. Keep the numbers large and legible.
* AC vent/compressor running while the person goes back to the thermostat.
* Do not over-act discomfort. Small believable behavior is stronger than “sweating in misery.”
C. DAVID CONFIRMS THE PROBLEM
Narrative job: transition from narrator-led discovery to a real human authority who confirms what the viewer just learned.
* Let David appear on camera at the start of his soundbite. Do not immediately cover him with B-roll.
* On his first appearance, a simple lower third can read: “David Patrick — Inventor of Spin360.”
* Use J/L cuts if helpful: stay on David for the important claim, then briefly cut to floor/mid-room visuals while his audio continues, then return to him.
* The goal is engagement + credibility, not just information delivery.
D. PRODUCT REVEAL / MECHANISM
Narrative job: Spin360 should now feel like the answer to a question the viewer already wants solved.
* Reveal with strong real Spin360 footage: clean full product, lifestyle placement, or product close-up.
* Use the existing After Effects airflow material to show air pulled from the top and bottom and sent outward around the room.
* Simple arrows / labels are enough. Do not turn this into a technical engineering demo.
* The exact product shot does not need to match each sentence. Use the real shot that best communicates the idea.
E. THE 78 / 71 → 73 / 73 / 73 PAYOFF
Narrative job: close the loop from the opening hook. This is one of the most important visual beats in the entire ad.
* If real footage of the test/gauges exists, prioritize it.
* Otherwise create a clean motion graphic with three levels/gauges: show the initial spread, then the three readings settling at 73°F.
* Hold the final 73 / 73 / 73 long enough to understand. Do not rush past the payoff.
* Do not present an AI-generated thermal-camera image as real proof.
F. CREDIBILITY BOUNDARY
Narrative job: David’s 105°F line makes the claim more believable by clearly defining what Spin360 does not do.
* Keep David visible for this line whenever possible.
* Supporting footage can simply show a hot room / exterior heat / temperature reading — not a dramatic “before vs after” cooling transformation.
* Optional restrained text: “Spin360 does not create cold air.” Then immediately return to the mix/circulation idea.
* This section should feel confident, not defensive.
G. CUSTOMER REVIEWS / SOCIAL PROOF
Narrative job: move from explanation to real-world outcomes without turning the edit into a generic testimonial montage.
* Use real review screenshots or real comment cards whenever available.
* Highlight only the exact phrase being heard; keep the rest visible enough that it still feels authentic.
* Alternate review proof with real product/lifestyle footage so the section keeps moving.
* Avoid fake energy-bill charts or invented savings graphics. The reviews are the proof.
H. CLOSE / POSITIONING
Narrative job: leave the viewer with one simple mental model, not another feature list.
* Real Spin360 in a believable room, AC/thermostat context, or a clean final product shot.
* Optional final headline: “MOVE THE COLD AIR YOU ALREADY PAID FOR.”
* The visual should reinforce: AC creates cold air; Spin360 helps move it through the room.
* End cleanly. No need for a flashy tech montage.
7. PRODUCT + MECHANISM RULES
* When Spin360 is recognizable, prefer real product footage. Avoid AI-generated hero/product shots if at all possible.
* Spin360 stays stationary; do not imply oscillation or invent product behavior that is not in the real footage.
* Use the existing top/bottom + outward airflow animation as the clearest mechanism explanation. Keep it simple.
* Do not show ice, fog, mist, or anything that implies Spin360 refrigerates the air.
* Do not imply that Spin360 lowers room temperature on its own. The story is mixing/moving the air that already exists.
* Do not fake thermal-camera footage, test data, or customer proof.
* If the perfect literal product shot does not exist, use the strongest real product material that supports the idea. Do not force an exact shot for every sentence.
8. AI FOOTAGE — USE SPARINGLY
AI is optional support, not the visual identity of this ad. If real footage, existing graphics, or a simple motion-design solution can communicate the idea, use those first.
GOOD USE OF AI (ONLY IF NEEDED)
	AVOID
	* A simple environmental bridge that does not need to prove anything.
* A generic bedroom/living-room moment when no believable real footage exists.
* A subtle non-product visual metaphor, only if it looks fully natural.
	* Spin360 itself.
* David.
* The 78/71/73 proof.
* Customer reviews or bills.
* Fake thermal-camera / scientific evidence.
* Any shot where AI artifacts would make the ad feel less trustworthy.
	9. OPTIONAL ON-SCREEN HEADLINES
Use text when it makes the idea easier to understand or gives a visual beat. Do not subtitle the strategy with giant text on every line.
ONE ROOM. TWO TEMPERATURES.
	YOUR THERMOSTAT SHOWS ONE NUMBER.
	78°F UP HIGH / 71°F DOWN LOW
	74 → 72 → 70
	73°F / 73°F / 73°F
	YOUR AC MAKES THE COLD AIR.
	SPIN360 HELPS MOVE IT.
	MOVE THE COLD AIR YOU ALREADY PAID FOR.
	10. SOUND + PACING
* Use a subtle documentary-style music bed. Curiosity first; slightly warmer/more reassuring after the solution and proof.
* Thermostat clicks, light AC hum, and restrained whooshes for airflow graphics can help comprehension. Keep SFX realistic.
* Use silence or a small music drop around key reveals (the 78/71 setup, product reveal, or 73/73/73 payoff) if it improves attention.
* Do not make the edit feel “busy” just to create retention. Retention should come from the story, the open loops, David, and the proof.
11. FINAL EDITOR NOTE
The story order is the strategy. The exact footage is not. Protect the hook, the reveal, David’s authority, the 78/71 → 73/73/73 payoff, and the final positioning. Within those boundaries, use judgment. If a suggested shot feels forced, fake, repetitive, or hurts pacing, replace it with something stronger.
	DELIVERABLE STRUCTURE: 3 visual hook variations + 1 shared body edit.
