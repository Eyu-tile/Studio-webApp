Script:
So... yeah. I got laid off.
Which is humiliating, because I’m a sheep. We had maybe the most secure job in human history.
People couldn’t sleep, they counted us. That was it. No degree, no résumé, no LinkedIn... you jump over a fence six or seven times and somebody passes out.
I thought I was set for life.
But apparently people don’t sleep like they used to, because now I show up to a bedroom and there’s traffic outside, somebody’s dog has been barking since midnight, the guy upstairs is walking around like he’s trying to stomp grapes through the floor...
and the human’s just lying there staring at the ceiling.
What am I supposed to do with that?
Jump harder?
So then everybody starts looking for something to cover all that noise. They’ve got apps, little white-noise machines, speakers playing rain sounds... one guy had something called “deep brown noise.”
I don’t even wanna know what that is.
But I get the idea. You put one steady sound in the room, and all those random little noises don’t stand out as much.
The problem is, some of this stuff sounds like a speaker going SHHHHHHHH at you for eight straight hours.
That’s not relaxing. That’s somebody trying to shut you up very slowly.
Then people use a fan, which honestly makes more sense to me because at least the sound is real. The blade is actually making it.
Except now you’ve got this stream of air aimed at the same part of your body all night, so you finally stop noticing the traffic... and wake up with one shoulder in December.
And this is where I lost my job.
Somebody made Spin360.
I know. I hate them too.
They designed the blade so, as it moves the air, it creates this smoother, steadier mechanical white noise. So you’re getting an actual sound from the blade itself — not a recording looping through a speaker — and that steady sound helps make the random stuff outside the room a lot less noticeable.
Traffic, barking, footsteps... all that stuff that keeps poking at your attention when you’re trying to fall asleep.
But here’s the really annoying part.
They didn’t stop at the sound.
A regular fan pushes air at one spot. This thing moves a softer breeze around the room, so instead of feeling like somebody left a leaf blower pointed at your pillow, there’s just this gentle movement through the space.
And... look, I’m saying this as somebody whose retirement plan depends on humans sleeping badly...
it feels good.
The room feels less still. Less closed-up. You’ve got that steady background sound, you’ve got soft air moving around the bedroom, and after a while the whole space just feels... easier to relax in.
More comfortable.
Like your body goes, “Oh. Okay. We can sleep here.”
Which is great for you.
Horrific for me.
Because now you don’t hear the dog as much, you’re not getting blasted in the face by a regular fan, the room feels better...
what am I bringing to the table?
Wool?
I’ve got three lambs at home, man.
One of them’s looking at college.
So yeah... Spin360.
If you sleep better tonight, just know a sheep paid the price.


1. Desire
 Surface Desire: Create a bedroom environment that makes it easier to relax, fall asleep, and stay asleep.
 Why Train: Fewer disturbances → more continuous sleep → wake up feeling more rested → feel better during the day.
Core Desire: Comfort / Control / Health
2. Avatar
 People whose sleep is disrupted by nighttime noise and who want a more consistent, comfortable sleep environment.
Experiences: traffic, neighbors, dogs, snoring, footsteps, household sounds, or other nighttime disturbances.
Behaviors: use fans, AC, air purifiers, digital white-noise machines, apps, speakers, or earplugs to mask nighttime noise; often keep searching when digital sound feels fake, looped, harsh, or distracting, when fans create unwanted airflow or inconsistent noise, or when the solution simply doesn’t mask enough disturbance.
3. Angle
 Nighttime noise makes it harder to relax, fall asleep, and stay asleep.
4. Mechanism
 Purpose-built blade-generated white noise.
Spin360’s blade is designed to move air smoothly instead of repeatedly chopping through it like a traditional fan. That produces a smoother, steadier mechanical white noise specifically suited for sleep — while the same blade creates a soft breeze that moves around the room rather than a harsh stream of air aimed at one spot.
5. Authority
 David Patrick — inventor of Spin360, who spent years developing the blade and airflow system behind the product.
Secondary Authority: real customers with firsthand experience using Spin360 for their sleep environment.
6. Awareness Level
 Problem Aware → Solution Aware
Problem Aware: they know traffic, neighbors, snoring, barking, or other nighttime sounds are disturbing their sleep, but may not yet see white noise as the solution.
Solution Aware: they already know about or use background sound, fans, white-noise machines, apps, AC, or similar solutions to mask nighttime noise.




7. Emotion — Valence + Intensity
 Negative Valence / Low-to-Medium Intensity
Primary emotions: annoyance, restlessness, frustration, and lack of control.
 Desired emotional state: calm, relief, comfort, and ease.
8. Differentiation / Positioning
 Enemy: Digital white-noise machines and apps that play artificial sound through a speaker, and traditional fans that chop the air, creating harsher fan noise and direct airflow.
Positioning: Spin360 is built to create a better kind of fan-generated white noise. Its purpose-built blade moves air smoothly instead of chopping it, producing a steadier, more soothing sleep sound while circulating a soft breeze around the room.
