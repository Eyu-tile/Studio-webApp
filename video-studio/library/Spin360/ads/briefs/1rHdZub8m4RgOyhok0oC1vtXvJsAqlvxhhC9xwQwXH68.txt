# Element

	Desire
	"I want to sleep like I used to." Fresh, moving air at night, like sleeping outside, so they wake up rested instead of stuffy and tired, without a fan blasting them in the face.
	Avatar
	Homeowners 45–65 sharing a closed bedroom. Curious about how things work (they stop for the guy with the drill) and skeptical of gadgets. Behavior: they already tried a box fan, the ceiling fan, the AC, and the room still feels stuffy. Belief to respect: "a fan is a fan, it just blows air at you."
	Angle
	One reason to buy: this strange shape moves air in your room the way it moves outside, and outdoor air is why Swedish babies sleep almost twice as long.
	Mechanism
	The patented shape pulls air in from the top and the bottom at the same time and sends it out 360°, like Saturn's rings. So it feels like a breeze, not a fan. The hook shows it: the blade pulls the smoke in.
	Proof
	The smoke pulled into the blade on camera, the Swedish outdoor-nap research in the body, and the inventor's track record.
	Authority
	David Patrick, the inventor who reinvented the wheel and got a deal on Shark Tank. Shown before the story starts, so the viewer trusts the shape before hearing why it matters.
	Awareness
	Hook: Unaware (what is this guy doing?). The bridge names the inventor and the shape. The body takes them from "my bedroom air feels dead" (Problem Aware) to "this fan moves air like outside" (Product Aware). Sophisticated market: the shape answers "how is yours different?".
	Emotion
	Curiosity (the smoke), "who is this guy?", surprise (he reinvented the wheel), then the Swedish babies story: wonder, then calm.
	Format
	Drill hook (no talking, on-screen text "what is this guy doing 😂") + a short AI narrator bridge (~13 s) + our winning Swedish Babies body, unchanged.
	Hooks
	3 drill hooks in real public city places, filmed like a stranger caught it on their phone: New York at night (the original), New York by day, Chicago by day.
	Offer
	No discount. The body is the version with the offer line already cut. It ends on "Go check it out."
