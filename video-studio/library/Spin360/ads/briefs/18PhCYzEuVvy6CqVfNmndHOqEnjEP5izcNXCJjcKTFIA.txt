Ad type: Ideation · visual-only (music + on-screen text, no voice) · Size: 9x16 · ~48 s · 1 ad.
The idea: an ad that sells without a single spoken word. We start outside, where the air moves and sleep feels easy. We cut to a closed bedroom where the air has stopped and she is awake at 3:07. Spin360 turns on, the air in her room starts moving like the air outside, and she falls asleep without noticing, sleeps through the whole night, and wakes up actually rested. Every shot has to make the viewer FEEL that, with no words. The on-screen text below only names what the viewer is already feeling.
	

How to read this doc: each shot has two pictures. Blender previs = the exact camera move, framing and timing (gray, rough, ignore the look). Inspiration = the look and mood we want. Generate each shot from the inspiration frame, and use the previs clip of that shot as a camera-movement reference only.

	1 · Hook
	1
	A real bed in the middle of a windy field at dusk. "Why is there a bed out there?"
	An image that should not exist stops the scroll. It also plants the promise of the whole ad: sleeping like you are outside. ("Bed outside" is a proven winner in this account.)
	2 · Desire
	2–4
	Wind in the grass, a curtain blowing at an open window, her face in the breeze.
	The viewer feels the air before we sell anything. This is the feeling we will bring indoors.
	3 · The problem
	5–6
	Hard cut to a closed bedroom at night. Dust frozen in the lamp light. 3:07 a.m., she is wide awake, nothing moves.
	The only dark moment. No words needed: still air, a sleepless woman, a clock. Everyone has lived it.
	4 · The mechanism (shown, not told)
	7–10
	Spin360 turns on, the frozen dust starts to drift. A ripple crosses the duvet and match-cuts into the same ripple on a lake.
	The match cut says it all: the air in her room now moves like the air outside. That is the product.
	5 · Sleep without noticing
	11–14
	She reads, her eyes get heavy, the book sinks. The whole night passes in one still time-lapse. Clock: 10:41 to 6:58.
	The transformation. She did not try to sleep, she just did, and stayed asleep. The clock is the proof.
	6 · Wake up rested
	15–18
	Sun on her face, eyes open clear, a small "I slept the whole night?" smile, a stretch, curtains open to the morning.
	The emotional payoff, and the loop closes: we end where we started, outside.
	7 · Product
	19
	Spin360 in morning light, space for the end card.
	Now the viewer wants to know what made that night possible.
	Emotion curve: up (outside, the breeze) → down (the stopped air, 3:07) → up higher than the start (the morning).
Music (guide): airy and open → drops out at shot 5 → returns at shot 7 → swells on the match cut → almost still during the time-lapse → full lift at the morning (shot 15).

	The three shots that matter most:
* Shot 1 (hook): it must read in one second as "a real bed, outside, in the wind". Bright enough, clear silhouette, moving sheet.
* Shots 8 + 9 (match cut): identical top-down framing, identical ripple direction and speed. The cut should feel invisible.
* Shot 13 (time-lapse): she does not move at all. Only the light changes. This shot is the promise of the ad.
Rules: no voice. The breeze is always pleasant, never cold. The Spin360 ring is off until shot 7. The product is always the real Spin360. The blue rings in the previs are only there to show the air: do not render them. Keep clean space in every shot for the on-screen text.
On-screen text
Few words, on purpose. The visuals sell; the text only names what the viewer already feels. Leave the shots marked "none" without text so the film can breathe. Style: white, centered, clean sans serif, one line at a time, in the safe area, with a soft fade in and out. Each card stays on screen for the whole shot (or across the shots listed).
