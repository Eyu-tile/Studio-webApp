	Desire
	Fall asleep in the best possible room: one temperature, nothing blowing on you, that fan sound. In their words (winter): "It's literally 80°F in the middle of winter while it's 35°F outside", "I wake up every morning so hot and dry", "The hubs complained about being cold", "I need the sound. I'll angle it so I barely feel it", "Stagnant air sucks. Having a slight breeze keeps you comfortable all night". The feeling we sell: blanket up to your chin, walls aren't there, bed outside on a perfect night.
	Avatar
	Women 40–65 who sleep with a fan on, or want to, and whose bedroom gets too warm up top when the heat is on. Window shut (noise, cold). Partner sometimes cold. They already point the fan at the wall so it doesn't blow on them.
	Angle
	One reason to buy: this is the first fan made for winter. The hook names the category; the body only gives the three facts and lets her conclude it herself (one temperature / nothing blows on you / the sound).
	Mechanism
	Warm air rises and cool air sinks, so the room is too warm up top and too cool down low. The patented blade pulls the warm air down and the cool air up and sends a soft breeze out all around the room, not at anyone. Result: one temperature, nothing on your face, a steady "shh" above speed 5.
	Proof
	Facts only, no test and no David by name in the body (nobody knows him until the end). The maker's measurement exists (ceiling 78, floor 71, whole room 73) and may go ON SCREEN as a label, never in the VO. Shark Tank footage is the proof of the maker at the end.
	Authority
	Closing line: "Made by the guy who reinvented the wheel on Shark Tank" (Shark Wheel; deal with Mark Cuban, Kevin O'Leary, Nick Woodman). Real Shark Tank footage on that line.
	Awareness
	Product aware for "fan", unaware for "winter fan". The hook is the quirk ("a fan, in winter?"); the body is problem → mechanism → feeling, three times, short.
	Positioning
	A new label on purpose: THE FIRST WINTER FAN. Not "a better fan". We never compare with regular fans, ceiling fans or competitors; we never say it heats or cools. The season word "winter" is deliberate in this ad.
	Emotion
	Curiosity ("a fan in winter?") → recognition (the room too warm up top, blanket on/off) → coziness (blanket up to the chin, walls gone) → calm (the shh) → trust (Shark Tank).

	Hooks

	Offer
	Try the Spin360 for 30 nights. If you don't love how your room feels, send it back. No discount, no price on screen.
	Production brief
Format: vertical 9:16, 40–45 s max. Female VO: warm, unhurried, like a friend telling you about something she loves. Not an announcer. Captions white, a few words at a time, lower third.
The feeling we sell: cozy. Blanket up to the chin, warm lamp light, snow outside, and the room feels alive instead of stuffy. Winter is in the picture (frost, snow, radiator, thick blankets, sweater) but the VO never says the product heats or cools anything.
One beat per reason: problem (2–3 s) → the product doing it (3 s) → how it feels (6–8 s). Three times. Then Shark Tank + end card. Calm cutting; this audience wants to feel the room, not be rushed.
Product: real shots of the Spin360 whenever you can (running, blade from above, in the corner of a bedroom). The house smoke footage shows the air pulled in from top and bottom and out in a ring. The Shark Tank cut is in LiveLonger_HOOK_6 at 01:24–01:32; prototypes in this folder.
Sound: under reason 3, use the real sound of the unit (speed 6–9), no music over it for those seconds. Music elsewhere: soft, warm, low.
Numbers only on screen, never in the VO. The 78/71/73 label is optional and only if the thermometers are in the picture.
