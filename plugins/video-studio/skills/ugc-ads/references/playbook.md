# Direct-response ad playbook (UGC and VSL)

Based on the house course "The Anatomy of Conversion" (editor onboarding), checked against platform
documentation and other sources (November 2026). Each rule is marked:
**[source]** backed by a platform or published study · **[house]** a house rule (a default to test, not a law) ·
**[corrected]** the course said something else; this is the checked version.

## Formats
| | VSL (video sales letter) | UGC (user-generated content) |
|---|---|---|
| Led by | the script: the words are the product | a person: phone-shot, to camera |
| Logic | problem → agitation → mechanism → proof → offer | "I had this problem, found this, here's what happened" |
| Earns | authority (an expert who is certain) | trust (a friend telling you) |
| Length | 60 s – 20+ min; social cuts 30–120 s | 15–60 s; TikTok in-feed 9–15 s recommended [source: TikTok] |
| Sells | higher-consideration items | impulse and low-friction items |
| Kill it by | dead air, doubtful delivery | polishing it into a brand film |

Direct response vs brand: judged by an action today (hook rate, hold rate, CTR, ROAS), payoff up front,
audience actively avoiding ads, gloss reads as "ad" = skip. TikTok's own guidance: "Go lo-fi (avoid
glossiness)", "Don't make ads. Make TikToks." [source: TikTok Creative Codes]

## Structure
- **Hook 0–3 s, ≈10% · Body ≈75% · CTA last 3–5 s, ≈15%** [house]. The first seconds carry most of the value:
  a Facebook/Nielsen study found 47% of a video campaign's value in the first 3 s and 74% in the first 10 s
  [source: Facebook/Nielsen 2015].
- Five layers in every block: script (argues), voice (persuades), music (sets the feeling), b-roll (proves),
  captions (carry it muted). Order of work: lock script timing → clean voice → b-roll → music → captions [house].

## Awareness → hook (Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*)  [source]
| Level | They know | Hook leads with |
|---|---|---|
| unaware | nothing yet | a story or a pattern interrupt; no product |
| problem | the pain, not the fix | the problem, in their words |
| solution | fixes exist | why this mechanism works where others fail |
| product | your product | proof, a demo, the objection they hold |
| most | ready | the offer and the reason to act now |
[corrected] the course asked "do they know the problem / solution / brand?"; use all five levels.

## Brief cascade (the same script, three different edits)  [house]
| Brief | Voice | Music | B-roll | Captions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 55+ · pain relief | calm, slower, reassuring, no slang | warm, soft, minimal percussion; tension → gentle release | real people that age, clear demo, good light | large, high contrast, 2–4-word phrases, slower; no trendy animation |
| 18–28 · beauty & confidence | fast, casual, first person, mid-story | trending, upbeat, drop on the reveal | phone-shot, handheld, mirror/bathroom realism, close-up texture | word-by-word, bouncy, one bright accent |
| 30–50 · save time | direct, confident, benefit-led | driving but neutral, steady, no vocals | screen recordings, before/after workflow, clean desk | 2–4-word phrases, clean sans, minimal effects |

Emotion → music: comfort = warm pads, slow, no percussion · pain relief = tension then release on the reveal ·
healing/wellness = soft piano, airy · performance/energy = driving drums 110–128 BPM [house]. Hook: an
impact/riser or a sudden silence; solution: drop or key change exactly on the reveal; proof: music recedes;
CTA: hold the energy, last note after the last word [house].

## Script
- 140–170 words/min; tighten by deleting words, not speeding the clip [house]. Caption reading guidance runs
  130–160 wpm (DCMP), so go to ~140 for older audiences [source: DCMP].
- Vary sentence length (long, long, short; cut and emphasise on the short one). One idea per line. Front-load
  the benefit. Keep specifics. No adjectives without a visual claim. Reorder if a later line hooks harder.
  One deliberate pause before the CTA [house].

## Voice
- UGC: keep breaths and a small stumble, slight room tone, cut on speech beats, energy climbs toward the result.
- VSL: no gap over 400 ms except a dramatic beat; clean, de-noised, compressed; downward inflection on claims;
  slow ~10% on the most important sentence [house].
- Loudness: ≈ −14 LUFS integrated, ≤ −1 dBTP, music ducked 12–18 dB under speech. −14 is YouTube's usual
  target; TikTok and Instagram publish none, so −14 is a sensible default, not a platform rule [corrected/source].

## B-roll
- Literal beats beautiful: show the sentence within the same second. Product in use, real hands, real places,
  the mechanism up close, reaction and result. Never generic stock that could sell anything [house].
- A new stimulus every 1.5–3 s (cut, push-in, text pop, overlay); static frames past ~4 s bleed retention unless
  the tension is deliberate [house].
- One world, one look: consistent colour, light direction, hands, location [house].
- **Before/after [corrected]:** for weight-loss (and health/supplement) ads Meta's Health & Wellness policy
  bans side-by-side before/after comparisons (fitness classes excepted); its personal-health policy shows a
  weight-loss before/after as non-compliant. For beauty, check the policy per ad. Show the result alone.

## Captions
- Captions are required, but sound matters too [corrected]: "most views start muted" comes from Facebook-feed
  research (Meta's tests: captions +12% view time), while on TikTok 88% of users say sound is essential
  [source: Meta 2016; TikTok/Kantar — vendor data].
- Style by audience: word-by-word for high-energy UGC; 2–4-word phrases for VSL and older audiences; full lines
  only for long-form. Heavy geometric sans (Montserrat Bold, Inter Black, Poppins SemiBold), one font. 5–8% of
  frame height on 1080×1920; ≤ 20 characters per line (DCMP's general limit is 32, two lines max). White with a
  6–10 px black stroke; one accent colour on the benefit word; never more than two colours [house/source].
- Frame-accurate: captions appear on the frame the word is spoken (vs captions builds them from word times).
- **Safe zones [corrected]:** the course said top 15% / bottom 20%. Meta Reels ads: keep the top 14%, bottom
  35% and 6% at each side clear (as restated by ad-spec guides; confirm on Meta's spec page). TikTok publishes
  safe-zone templates per format rather than fixed numbers; use them. The tools use these per `--preset`.

## Compliance (not in the course; required)
- **FTC rule on consumer reviews and testimonials** (in force Oct 21, 2024): no fake or AI-invented reviewers or
  testimonials, no bought reviews; civil penalties for knowing violations [source: FTC].
- **Endorsements:** a paid creator's post is an endorsement; the material connection must be disclosed
  (FTC Act). Use the platform's paid-partnership label and/or #ad.
- **Claims** need substantiation (health and supplement claims especially); keep the evidence with the brief.
- **Platform policy:** weight-loss ads 18+ only, no body shaming (Meta); check category rules before shooting.

## Metrics (define once, use the same formulas everywhere)
- **Hook rate** = 3-second video plays ÷ impressions (Meta's 3-second plays: 3 s or 97% of a shorter video,
  replays excluded) [source: Meta, via Motion/Vaizle].
- **Hold rate** = ThruPlays ÷ 3-second plays (a ThruPlay = completion if < 15 s, else 15 s). There are at least
  three definitions in circulation; this is the most common. Neither is a native Ads Manager column: build
  custom metrics with the formula in the description [source].
- Creative fatigue is normal; plan several hooks per body and rotate [source: TikTok].

## Delivery checklist
Automated by `vs ads qa` / `vs review --brief`: spec 1080×1920 and length; frame 1 has motion/face/product,
no logo or fade; first words on screen ≤ 0.5 s; hook ≤ 3 s; a new visual at least every 3 s; safe zones;
one CTA held ≥ 2.5 s; −14 LUFS / ≤ −1 dBTP; music ducked; consistent look; compliance from the brief.
By hand: every claim has a matching visual on the beat; captions checked at 0.5×; watched once fully muted
and once on a phone at arm's length in the app; named to the convention.
